### 🎬 **180-Degree Rule — Grammar of Cinema**
**Definition:**  
In filmmaking, the **180-degree rule** is a spatial guideline that maintains consistent screen direction between characters or elements in a scene.  

---

### 🧭 **How It Works**
Imagine a straight line — called the **axis of action** — drawn between two characters or a character and an object of focus.  
The camera must stay on **one side of this line** during all shots in a scene.  

- Character A always appears on the **left** of the frame.  
- Character B always appears on the **right**.  
This helps the audience instantly understand who’s facing whom and preserves spatial clarity.  

---

### 🎞️ **If You Break It**
Crossing the line (the **“jumping the line”** mistake) flips the characters’ positions on screen — left becomes right, right becomes left — causing **visual confusion** unless done deliberately for effect.  

---

### 🧩 **Why It Matters**
- Maintains audience orientation in space  
- Builds visual coherence between shots  
- Forms the backbone of **cinematic grammar** (alongside shot–reverse shot, eyeline match, match on action, etc.)  

---

### 🎭 **When Directors Break It Intentionally**
Some directors *break* the 180° rule for psychological effect:
- **Stanley Kubrick** (to unsettle viewers)
- **Jean-Luc Godard** (to challenge cinematic convention)
- **Christopher Nolan** (for temporal/spatial distortion)
