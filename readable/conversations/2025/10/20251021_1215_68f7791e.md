### 🌶️ General Indian Curry Order (North & South Indian Base)
**1️⃣ Oil + Whole Spices (if used)**
- Heat oil.
- Add bay leaf, cumin, mustard seeds, etc. (optional)
- Sauté until they splutter.

**2️⃣ Ginger-Garlic Paste**
- Add **ginger-garlic paste** to the hot oil (after whole spices).
- Sauté on medium flame till raw smell goes (about 30–60 seconds).
- This gives depth and removes bitterness.

**3️⃣ Onion + Salt**
- Add **finely chopped onions**.
- Add a **pinch of salt** (helps onions brown faster).
- Sauté till **golden brown** for rich curries  
  or **lightly pink** for simple sabzis.

**4️⃣ Tomatoes**
- Add **chopped or pureed tomatoes** next.
- Cook till oil separates (this means masala is cooked properly).
- You can cover for 2–3 mins to soften them faster.

**5️⃣ Dry Spices**
- Add your powdered spices once tomato softens:
  - **Turmeric powder**
  - **Chilli powder**
  - **Coriander powder**
  - (Add cumin powder, garam masala, etc. if recipe calls)
- Stir and roast the masala 1–2 mins till oil starts separating again.

**6️⃣ Main Ingredient**
- Add your vegetables, chicken, paneer, or lentils.
- Mix to coat with masala.

**7️⃣ Water / Stock**
- Add water as needed, simmer till cooked.
- Adjust salt again near the end.

**8️⃣ Fresh herbs (like coriander leaves, curry leaves)**
- Add at the very end for aroma and color.

## 🍳 Why the Order Depends on Dish Type

Different dishes release **moisture, fat, and flavor** at different stages.  
That changes **when and how long** to cook onions, tomatoes, and masalas.

Let’s break it down:

---

### 🥦 1️⃣ **Vegetarian Curries (Veg gravies, paneer, dal, etc.)**
- Veggies have **less fat** and **milder flavor**.
- So, you need to **build flavor from the base** — i.e. onions, tomatoes, and dry spices.  
- You sauté onions till golden, cook tomato till oil separates, then add turmeric/chilli/coriander powders.  
- This slow cooking (“bhunao”) gives the curry its **rich masala taste**.

👉 **Key:** Masala needs to be fully cooked *before* adding the vegetable.  
Otherwise, the dish tastes raw or flat.

---

### 🍗 2️⃣ **Non-Veg Curries (Chicken, mutton, fish)**
- Meat has **natural fat and strong flavor**.
- Ginger-garlic paste is more important here (to remove raw smell).
- Often, meat is added **earlier**, before tomatoes — so it browns and absorbs the spice base.  
  (This step is called *sealing* the meat.)

👉 **Order usually:**  
Oil → Whole spices → Onion → Ginger-garlic → Meat → Salt → Tomato → Dry spices → Water → Slow cook  

- If you add tomato too early, the acid prevents meat from browning properly.
- That’s why non-veg recipes slightly alter the order.

---

### 🌶️ 3️⃣ **Dry Fries / Stir-fries (Potato fry, mushroom fry, egg fry)**
- These are **dry dishes** — you don’t want watery gravy.
- So, you skip or minimize tomato and use **less masala paste**.
- Onion and ginger-garlic are just lightly sautéed before adding the main ingredient.

👉 **Order:**  
Oil → Mustard/cumin → Onion + Curry leaves → Ginger-garlic → Veggies → Salt → Dry masala → Toss till dry/crispy.

---

### 🌾 4️⃣ **Dal / Lentil Dishes**
- Here the masala is cooked separately (*tadka*), then added to boiled dal.
- You fry ginger-garlic, chilli, onion, and tomato in oil/ghee with dry spices —  
  then pour it over the dal for aroma.

👉 Order:  
Ghee → Mustard/cumin → Garlic → Onion → Tomato → Spices → Pour over dal.

---

### 💡 In Short

| Dish Type | When to Add Masala | Tomato Role | Purpose |
|------------|--------------------|--------------|----------|
| Veg curry | After onion browns | Main body | Build flavor |
| Non-veg curry | After meat is sealed | Adds tang | Balance fat |
| Fry | After veggie softens | Often skipped | Keep dry |
| Dal | Separate tempering | Finisher | Aroma & color |

## 🥔 What “Sealing Step” Means (and Why You Skip It in Veg Curries)

### 🍗 In Non-Veg Curries
When cooking meat (like chicken or mutton), there’s a **crucial step** after frying onions and ginger-garlic:

👉 **You add raw meat before tomatoes**,  
and **fry it on medium-high flame for 3–5 minutes**.

That’s called **“sealing”** — because you’re:
- lightly browning the outside of the meat,  
- letting it release and reabsorb its natural fat and juices,  
- locking in flavor before adding moisture (tomato or water).  

This process gives depth and prevents the meat from becoming bland or rubbery later.

---

### 🥦 In Vegetable Curries
Vegetables (like potato, beans, carrot, peas, cauliflower) don’t need this sealing step because:
- They have **no fat to sear**.  
- Their flavors develop **from the masala**, not from browning.  
- They also **cook faster** and can get mushy if fried too long early.

So in veg curries, you **skip sealing** and instead:

1️⃣ Fully cook your **masala base first**  
 → onions + ginger-garlic + tomatoes + dry spices  
 → until oil separates and masala is aromatic.  

2️⃣ Then **add chopped vegetables** and **toss** to coat them evenly in that masala.  

3️⃣ Finally, add water, cover, and simmer till the veggies are tender.
