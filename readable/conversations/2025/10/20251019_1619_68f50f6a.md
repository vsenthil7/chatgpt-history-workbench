Short answer: there’s hype around both, but AI is delivering broader, faster, and more measurable ROI than blockchain in most markets right now.

### How they’re different (in practice)

- **Core value**
  - **AI:** Turns data into predictions, content, decisions, and automation. Directly boosts productivity, revenue, and user experience.
  - **Blockchain:** Provides shared, tamper-evident records and programmable asset rails. Shines where multiple parties don’t fully trust each other.

- **Adoption breadth**
  - **AI:** Horizontal—sales, support, marketing, software, ops, analytics, healthcare, education, creative tools. Low barrier to pilot (APIs, SaaS).
  - **Blockchain:** Narrower—DeFi, digital assets/tokens, provenance, cross-org settlements, selective supply-chain and identity pilots.

- **Time-to-value**
  - **AI:** Weeks to first wins (assistants, copilots, forecasting, doc automation).
  - **Blockchain:** Longer cycles—governance, consortium alignment, compliance, key management, on/off-ramp issues.

- **Unit economics**
  - **AI:** Opex (compute/API). Costs offset by productivity/revenue gains; models can be right-sized.
  - **Blockchain:** Network costs + integration + compliance. ROI depends on multi-party participation and network effects.

- **Tooling & talent**
  - **AI:** Rich tooling (APIs, vector DBs, orchestration, evals); large talent pool.
  - **Blockchain:** Good but more specialized; security/keys/smart-contract risks require niche expertise.

- **Regulatory surface**
  - **AI:** Data privacy, IP, safety/AI Act style compliance.
  - **Blockchain:** Securities/AML/KYC, custody, tax—often heavier.

### When AI clearly wins
- You want **prediction, classification, generation, summarization**, copilots, or workflow automation.
- You have **owned data** and can measure lift (CSAT, lead conversion, backlog burn-down, time-to-close).
- You need **personalization** or **unstructured-data** leverage (docs, emails, PDFs, images).

### When blockchain clearly wins
- **Multi-organization workflows** where no single party should own the database (e.g., settlement, inter-agency registries).
- **Provenance and auditability** that must be verifiable by outsiders (land records, carbon credits, high-value assets).
- **Programmable assets/markets** (tokens, stablecoin rails) or **selective disclosure** credentials.

### Quick decision rubric
- Does the problem require **better predictions/automation**? → **AI**
- Does it require a **shared, immutable ledger** across parties with limited trust? → **Blockchain**
- Both? Use AI to extract/score data, and write **key events** to a ledger for audit.

### Examples tailored to public-sector & data/ops
- **Procurement & audits (multi-dept):**  
  - AI: Extract line items from PDFs, flag anomalies, generate summaries.  
  - Blockchain: Record tender milestones & payments immutably across departments/vendors.
- **Irrigation/water projects:**  
  - AI: Forecast demand, detect leaks from sensor time-series, summarize FMRs.  
  - Blockchain: Community-visible ledger of disbursements and WUA payments.
- **Warehouse/logistics:**  
  - AI: Slotting optimization, demand forecasting, pick-path copilots, document bots.  
  - Blockchain: Chain-of-custody for sensitive goods and automated claims with oracles.

### Risks & limitations
- **AI:** Hallucinations, data leakage, compliance with sensitive data, ongoing model costs, eval/monitoring discipline required.
- **Blockchain:** Governance deadlock, throughput/latency trade-offs, key/custody risk, unclear regulation, integration friction.

### Bottom line
- **Market using AI is “better” today** for most orgs because it maps directly to productivity and decision quality with faster payback.  
- **Blockchain is valuable** where trust, provenance, and multi-party coordination are the primary constraints—not as a general compute or analytics layer.
