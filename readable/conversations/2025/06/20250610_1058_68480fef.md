If you're concerned about security and privacy when receiving calls — especially from unknown or suspicious numbers — it's good to be cautious, though it's important to clarify:

**Just receiving or picking up a phone call cannot “hack” your phone directly.** But certain risks do exist:

---

### 🔒 **Realistic Threats to Be Aware Of**
1. **Phishing via Voice (Vishing):**  
   Scammers may try to trick you into giving personal info (bank OTPs, passwords, etc.).

2. **Social Engineering:**  
   Attackers may pretend to be from your bank, employer, or service provider to manipulate you.

3. **Spyware Links via SMS/WhatsApp:**  
   After the call, they might send a link. Clicking it can install spyware if your phone is unprotected.

4. **SIM Swap Attempts:**  
   Sometimes calls are part of a larger scheme to gather data and then perform a SIM swap.

---

### ⚠️ **Red Flags — Be Extra Cautious If:**
- The number is unknown, international, or spoofed (looks like your number).
- The caller asks for OTPs, PINs, or personal details.
- They claim urgency: “your bank account is in danger” / “police case against you.”
- You hear odd noises or silent calls repeatedly — possible reconnaissance.

---

### ✅ **Safe Practices Before/After Picking a Call**
- **Don't share personal info over calls.**
- **Use caller ID apps (e.g., Truecaller)** to spot fraud.
- **Block/report** scammy numbers.
- **Keep your phone updated** with the latest security patches.
- **Use antivirus/anti-malware apps** if you're especially concerned.

---

### 🛑 Not True: Myths
- **"Just answering a call can hack your phone."** → FALSE.
- Modern smartphones (Android/iOS) have built-in security to prevent remote takeovers via calls alone.

---

If you suspect a scam or feel uneasy, **just hang up** and do not engage further. Let me know if you want to check a suspicious number or call type.
