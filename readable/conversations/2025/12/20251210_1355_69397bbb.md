## ✅ What the new law does

- Under this regime, if an umbrella company fails to pay over required deductions (PAYE tax / Class 1 NI), then not only the umbrella but also other parties “up the chain” — typically the recruitment agency (or MSP) that placed the worker, or, if no agency, the end client — can be held **jointly and severally liable** for the full unpaid amount. citeturn0search1turn0search9turn0search16  
- The liability is strict: there is no “reasonable care” or “I didn’t know” defence. Even if the agency or client genuinely believed the umbrella was compliant, they can be pursued. citeturn0search14turn0search18turn0search12

## 🚨 Why this matters — and who is affected

### Agencies / MSPs / Recruitment firms  
- They face a **major increase in risk**: if a non-compliant umbrella company fails to remit PAYE/NIC, the agency could end up paying — potentially large amounts, even years after work was done. citeturn0search4turn0search7turn0search16  
- The practical upshot: many agencies may reconsider whether to continue using umbrella companies; some may bring payroll “in house” or otherwise restructure how they engage contractors to avoid liability. citeturn0search12turn0search20turn0search19  
- Due diligence becomes crucial: agencies will need strong, documented evidence that any umbrella company they use is fully compliant — and regularly monitored. citeturn0search9turn0search14turn0search20  

### End Clients / Hirers

- For larger clients or MSP-managed supply chains: there will likely be increased scrutiny of supplier lists (“preferred supplier lists”), stricter onboarding and compliance checking of umbrella providers, and possibly fewer umbrella providers willing to be engaged. citeturn0search9turn0search7

## 🔧 What organisations should do now (pre-April 2026)  

Given the law comes into force in a few months, any business involved in hiring contractors — whether as agency, MSP, umbrella provider or end user — should be taking action now:

- **Map your supply chain**: understand exactly who you contract with, which umbrella companies are used, and who is responsible for payroll.  
- **Vet umbrella companies thoroughly**: check their compliance history, evidence of tax and NI payments, financial robustness — and don’t just rely on a “preferred supplier list” from a partner.  
- **Update contracts and agreements**: ensure that compliance obligations and indemnities are clearly defined; consider requiring the umbrella to demonstrate compliance (payslip-level detail, PAYE reference, remittance history).  
- **Consider alternative models**: for some agencies or clients, running payroll themselves — or using verified “fully compliant” umbrella providers — may become preferable to mitigate risk.  
- **Monitor regulatory developments**: further guidance (for example, on what constitutes a “purported umbrella” or acceptable due diligence) may emerge — stay alert for updates from HMRC or legal advisers.
