## STEP-1 — MODULE DEFINITION (LOCK CANDIDATE)

### Problem Frame (Why GSNSDM Exists)

Early-stage migrants, students, and dependent workers routinely face:

- Ambiguous job messages
- Informal agents or intermediaries
- Requests for unfamiliar documents
- Pressure-based timelines (“urgent”, “last slot”, “act now”)

The risk is **not ignorance**, but **information asymmetry under stress**.

GSNSDM exists to reduce *panic-driven decisions*, not to confirm truth.

---

### GSNSDM — Working Definition (Draft)

> **GSNSDM is a self-guided safety reflection module that helps users slow down, recognise common risk patterns in work-related messages or document requests, and understand safer next steps — without confirming scams, validating documents, or issuing judgement.**

This definition is:
- Non-authoritative
- Non-predictive
- Non-enforcement
- User-centred

---

### One-Line Truth (Candidate Canonical Sentence)

> *A descriptive safety lens that helps users reflect on potential risk signals in offers or document requests, without deciding what is real or fake.*

(We will lock this in Step-4, after non-claims.)

---

### What GSNSDM IS (Positive Scope)

GSNSDM **IS**:
- A reflection and awareness aid
- Pattern-based (not fact-based)
- User-initiated and user-controlled
- Calm, non-fear-inducing
- Suitable for demo and judges

GSNSDM **HELPS USERS**:
- Pause before acting
- Notice common red flags
- Understand why something *might* feel unsafe
- Identify safer next actions (e.g., seek advice, verify independently)

---

### What GSNSDM Is NOT (Early Guardrail)

GSNSDM is **NOT**:
- A scam detector
- A document validator
- A reporting or enforcement tool
- A legal or immigration advisor
- A replacement for official verification

(Explicit non-claims will be locked in Step-4.)

---

## STEP-2 — RISK / SCAM CATEGORIES (DESCRIPTIVE ONLY)

These categories **do not assert fraud**.  
They describe *common patterns* reported by users in work-related interactions.

---

### CATEGORY-1: Identity & Representation Ambiguity

**Description:**  
Unclear or shifting identity of the person or organisation making contact.

**Common Signals (Non-Exhaustive):**
- Sender avoids verifiable organisational details
- Name, role, or company changes mid-conversation
- Uses generic titles (“HR Team”, “Agent”, “Coordinator”)
- Contact via informal channels for formal matters

**Why It Matters:**  
Unclear identity increases misunderstanding and misuse risk.

---

### CATEGORY-2: Urgency & Pressure Framing

**Description:**  
Language designed to reduce reflection time.

**Common Signals:**
- “Last chance”, “urgent”, “today only”
- Artificial deadlines without explanation
- Emotional pressure (fear of loss or exclusion)
- Discouragement of outside consultation

**Why It Matters:**  
Pressure reduces decision quality, even in legitimate contexts.

---

### CATEGORY-3: Document Misuse or Overreach

**Description:**  
Requests for documents that feel excessive, poorly explained, or premature.

**Common Signals:**
- Requests before formal offer or contract
- Vague explanation of purpose or storage
- Requests for full originals instead of partials
- Repeated resubmission requests without reason

**Why It Matters:**  
Documents can be reused beyond their stated purpose.

---

### CATEGORY-4: Fee, Payment, or Deposit Requests

**Description:**  
Requests involving money tied to access, processing, or prioritisation.

**Common Signals:**
- Upfront fees for interviews, offers, or processing
- Requests to pay individuals rather than organisations
- Unclear refund or receipt process
- Pressure to pay quickly

**Why It Matters:**  
Financial requests change the risk profile of any interaction.

---

### CATEGORY-5: Channel & Process Inconsistency

**Description:**  
Mismatch between expected professional process and actual interaction.

**Common Signals:**
- Official-sounding requests via personal messaging apps
- Poor alignment between stated organisation and process used
- Requests to bypass standard procedures
- Conflicting instructions from different contacts

**Why It Matters:**  
Process inconsistency often signals misunderstanding or risk.

---

### CATEGORY-6: Information Withholding or Evasion

**Description:**  
Avoidance of reasonable clarification questions.

**Common Signals:**
- Non-answers to basic “why” or “how” questions
- Deflection when asked for confirmation details
- Repeated reassurance without evidence
- Hostility to clarification

**Why It Matters:**  
Transparency reduces risk; evasion increases uncertainty.

All inputs are **voluntary, user-declared, and unverified**.

### INPUT-TYPE A: Interaction Summary (Text)

User may describe:
- Message received (offer, request, follow-up)
- Who contacted them (as claimed)
- Context (job, training, placement, document request)

**Constraints:**
- Free-text only
- No requirement to paste full messages
- No obligation to name organisations

---

### INPUT-TYPE B: Document Request Description

User may indicate:
- Type of document requested (e.g., ID, qualification, address proof)
- Stage at which it was requested
- How it was requested (email, message, in person)

**Constraints:**
- No document validation
- No assessment of legitimacy
- No storage requirement

---

### INPUT-TYPE C: Payment or Fee Mention (Optional)

User may note:
- Whether money was requested
- Nature of the payment (fee, deposit, processing)
- How payment was requested (method, timing)

**Constraints:**
- No amount analysis
- No payment advice

---

### INPUT-TYPE D: User Concern Flags (Self-Declared)

User may self-select:
- “Feels rushed”
- “Feels unclear”
- “Feels unusual”
- “Not sure why this is needed”

These are **feelings, not signals**.

---

## STEP-3 — SAFE OUTPUT RESPONSE TYPES

GSNSDM responds in **four non-decisive layers**.

---

### RESPONSE-TYPE 1: Pattern Reflection

GSNSDM may:
- Map user-described elements to **risk categories**
- Use neutral language (“This resembles patterns where…”)
- Highlight *why* such patterns can create risk

❌ No “this is a scam”
❌ No probability or score

---

### RESPONSE-TYPE 2: Clarifying Questions (Optional)

GSNSDM may suggest:
- Questions the user could ask the sender
- Information the user may want to request
- Areas where clarification usually helps

All phrased as **options**, not instructions.

---

### RESPONSE-TYPE 3: Safer Next-Step Options

GSNSDM may suggest:
- Slowing down before acting
- Independent verification paths (without naming authorities)
- Discussing with trusted advisors or official channels

No single “correct” action is prescribed.

---

### RESPONSE-TYPE 4: Emotional De-escalation

GSNSDM may:
- Acknowledge uncertainty or stress
- Normalize hesitation
- Reinforce that pausing is acceptable

No fear amplification.

---

## FALSE-POSITIVE HANDLING (CRITICAL)

GSNSDM must explicitly state:

- Legitimate interactions can show one or more patterns
- Patterns ≠ fraud
- Context matters
- Users retain full agency

This statement will be **mandatory** in final output.
