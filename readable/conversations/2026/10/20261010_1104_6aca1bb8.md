Also, using AI isn't itself what prevents monetisation. YouTube says required AI disclosure by itself doesn't reduce eligibility to earn money. citeturn0search0

# One thing I would NOT do initially

Don't automate publishing on day one.

Automate everything until:

```text
READY_FOR_PUBLISH
```

Then **you approve it**.

After perhaps 30–50 successful episodes, we can determine which controls can safely disappear.

The dangerous part isn't technically uploading a video automatically.

The dangerous part is automatically publishing:

- false breaking news,
- misunderstood research,
- copyrighted material,
- hallucinated product capabilities,
- bad code,
- security vulnerabilities,
- defamatory claims,
- or 20 nearly identical videos.

One bad automated publication can cost considerably more than the few minutes saved by eliminating approval.

# 4. Your alert mechanism should be first-class

Completely agree.

A single-person operation cannot mean:

> one person watches 25 automated systems all day.

The automation must operate silently and involve you **only when your judgement/access is required**.

I'd define three states:

```text
GREEN
System resolves automatically.

AMBER
System retries / switches provider /
uses fallback / records degradation.

RED
Human intervention required.
```

For example:

```text
🚨 HUMAN ACTION REQUIRED

Pipeline: Episode #037
Stage: Repository build
Problem: GitHub authentication expired

Automatic attempts:
✓ retry
✓ token refresh
✓ alternate connection
✗ all failed

Impact:
Episode cannot progress to build stage.

Required action:
Reconnect GitHub.

Everything before this stage is preserved.

[RETRY AFTER FIX]
```

Other RED conditions:

- MCP installation required
- OAuth permission required
- payment required
- API quota exhausted
- CAPTCHA
- account verification
- publication policy concern
- three AIs cannot resolve factual disagreement
- security vulnerability
- copyright concern
- build repeatedly failing
- unexpected spend threshold exceeded

**You become the exception handler, not the workflow engine.**

That's essential for a one-person company.

# Proposed Product Experience — V001

## 1. Customer's Starting Point

The customer should not need to understand agents, MCP, APIs, models, workflows or automation platforms.

The first question is simply:

**What outcome do you want?**

Example:

> I want to automatically promote my bakery during upcoming festivals using social media, WhatsApp and my website.

The customer may also select an operating preference:

- Free
- Economy
- Balanced
- Best

This is a budget/quality preference, not a requirement to understand individual AI providers.

---

## 2. The System Does Not Immediately Build Anything

The platform first interprets the requested business outcome.

It researches available capabilities and determines:

- what the customer appears to need;
- what existing software can already perform the work;
- which free and paid capabilities are available;
- estimated monetary cost;
- expected human effort;
- expected execution time;
- important risks and assumptions;
- what information or permissions will eventually be required.

At this stage the system is planning, not committing expensive execution.

---

## 3. Outcome Preview

Before building or configuring the real solution, the system creates a low-cost representation of the proposed outcome.

Depending on the requested outcome, this could be:

- written example;
- wireframe;
- clickable UI;
- simulated application;
- short screen walkthrough;
- sample report;
- sample voice conversation;
- sample marketing content;
- short video.

The objective is not to create an impressive marketing video.

The objective is:

**Allow the customer to understand what we intend to deliver before we spend significant money or effort delivering it.**

---

## 4. Example Preview

For the bakery example, the preview could demonstrate:

1. The system notices an upcoming festival.
2. A campaign opportunity is proposed.
3. Sample social-media content is displayed.
4. A WhatsApp campaign is shown.
5. A proposed website banner is shown.
6. Estimated campaign cost is displayed.
7. The owner approves or changes the proposal.
8. Publishing occurs.
9. Results are subsequently measured.
10. The system recommends what to continue, modify or stop.

The preview is explicitly marked as a **simulation/proposal**, not as evidence that the system has already been implemented.

---

## 5. Written Contract Accompanies the Preview

Every preview must have a corresponding written representation.

The written representation explains:

### What You Will Receive

For example:

- festival campaign detection;
- social-media content;
- WhatsApp campaign;
- website update;
- publishing automation;
- performance measurement;
- recommendations based on results.

### What You Must Provide

For example:

- business information;
- logo and brand material;
- approval rules;
- account permissions;
- budget;
- approximately 10–15 minutes of human review per week.

### What We Are Assuming

Assumptions must be explicitly stated rather than silently converted into requirements.

---

## 6. Tools and Costs

The customer can inspect which capabilities are proposed.

Example:

| Purpose | Proposed Capability | Free/Paid | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Research | Research provider | Free/Paid | £X |
| Copy | Language model | Free/Paid | £X |
| Images | Image capability | Free/Paid | £X |
| Video | Video capability | Free/Paid | £X |
| Workflow | Existing automation | Open source/Paid | £X |
| Publishing | Platform APIs | Free/Paid | £X |
| Monitoring | Existing capability | Free/Paid | £X |

These are not permanently fixed providers.

The capability-selection system should choose according to current requirements, evidence, price, reliability and customer constraints.

Commercial partnerships must be disclosed separately and must not secretly determine ranking.

---

## 7. Alternatives

The customer should be able to compare approaches.

For example:

### Free

Uses free/open-source/local capabilities wherever practical.

Lowest monetary cost.

May require more time, reduced quality or additional human work.

### Economy/Balanced

Uses paid intelligence only where evidence suggests it creates enough additional value.

### Premium

Uses higher-quality capabilities and additional independent review where appropriate.

The system should explain the expected difference rather than merely claiming that Premium is better.

---

## 8. Customer Can Challenge the Proposal

Before implementation the customer can say:

> Add email marketing.

or:

> Remove video because it is too expensive.

or:

> I want everything open source.

or:

> I can spend £20/month maximum.

or:

> I don't want my business data sent to external AI providers.

The proposal, preview, estimated cost and written contract are regenerated.

This continues until the customer understands and accepts the intended outcome.

---

## 9. Approval Creates the UAT Contract

When the customer approves the preview, the approved behaviour becomes the basis of User Acceptance Testing.

Example:

**Given** a festival campaign has been approved,

**When** publishing begins,

**Then** the approved social-media channels should publish successfully,

**And** the website should receive the approved update,

**And** individual channel failure must not corrupt successful channels,

**And** failures must be visible and recoverable.

Therefore:

**Preview → written behaviour → acceptance criteria → implementation → automated verification**

The preview and implementation must not become two unrelated artifacts.

---

## 10. Build Only After Approval

Only after approval does the platform assemble the real solution.

It should preferentially reuse existing capabilities rather than recreate them.

Possible components include:

- deterministic scripts;
- existing open-source software;
- APIs;
- MCP servers;
- AI models;
- workflow engines;
- SaaS tools;
- local models;
- our own components;
- human action where automation is economically unjustified.

The objective is not maximum AI.

The objective is the **minimum-cost, minimum-human-effort reliable combination capable of achieving the agreed outcome.**

---

## 11. Verification

The finished implementation is tested against the approved UAT contract.

A system should not be considered complete merely because code was generated successfully.

The question is:

**Does the delivered behaviour match what the customer approved?**

Where it does not, the difference is explicitly reported.

---

## 12. Operation and Measurement

After deployment, the platform measures actual results.

For the example:

- £2.87 spent;
- 17 leads;
- 6 orders;
- £184 revenue;
- human intervention;
- provider/API usage;
- failures;
- retries;
- AI cost;
- deterministic-processing cost.

The system may then recommend:

> Continue WhatsApp because measured ROI is positive.

> Reduce Instagram because spend is producing insufficient results.

This closes the loop between the original business outcome and actual evidence.

---

# Transparency Principle

The customer should never receive only:

> 637 credits consumed.

They should be able to understand:

- what consumed them;
- which capability performed the work;
- why that capability was selected;
- what it actually cost where measurable;
- what free/cheaper alternatives existed;
- what human effort was avoided;
- what result was produced.

AI consumption should be understandable like a detailed financial statement.

---

# Independent Verification Principle

The customer owns the written description of the proposed solution.

They are free to copy it into ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, another AI system, send it to a consultant, or give it to their technical team and ask:

> Is this proposal sensible?

> Is this overpriced?

> Are there cheaper alternatives?

> What risks have they missed?

> Can I build this myself?

The platform should not depend on information asymmetry to retain customers.

If an independent system identifies a better approach, the customer should be able to bring that recommendation back and ask the platform to reconsider the plan.

---

# Core Product Promise

The product is not:

**"We have lots of AI agents."**

It is:

**Tell us the business outcome you want. We will show you what we propose before expensive execution, explain the cost and alternatives, let you challenge it, assemble existing capabilities where possible, execute only after approval, verify the delivered result against what you accepted, and show whether the result was economically worthwhile.**

---

This storyboard and document are themselves our first Visual UAT.

Before implementation, we should answer:

**If this were the real product, is this the experience we want a first-time customer to have?**

Anything that is wrong should be changed here before becoming architecture or code.

# Your statistics idea changes provider selection

This is important.

We shouldn't primarily ask customers:

> Which AI would you like?

Most won't know.

And we shouldn't primarily use internet popularity either.

Our routing should gradually learn from **actual platform outcomes**.

Suppose we've accumulated 18,420 comparable research tasks:

```text
Research capability — last 30 days

                 Success   Avg Cost   Human Fix   Outcome
Provider A        96.2%      £0.18       7.1%      91
Provider B        94.8%      £0.07      11.3%      87
Provider C        97.1%      £0.31       4.2%      94
Free model        89.7%      £0.00      19.8%      78
```

Now **Balanced** might automatically choose Provider A.

Free chooses the free model.

Best chooses C.

But crucially, these statistics need segmentation. A provider may be excellent at English research and poor at Tamil transcription, or strong at coding but expensive for classification.

So we learn:

> **provider × capability × task type × complexity × outcome**

rather than declaring one provider universally best.

## Therefore I would discard the V001 customer UI

**Keep the content. Kill the interface.**

The 12-panel storyboard remains useful as our internal thinking document, but I would **not build that screen**.

Our V002 principle becomes:

> **Chat is the product entrance. Outcome is the artifact. Permission controls spending/action. The right panel contains evidence. Complexity appears only when requested.**

And there's a second principle:

> **Don't make customers choose AI tools. Ask what outcome they want and what constraints matter. We choose the capability using measured evidence; they can inspect, override or demand Free/Best whenever they want.**

This also means we don't need another expensive conceptual video yet. **First we should create one V002 screen showing this exact chat + collapsible Outcome panel and validate that single experience.** After that, the 45–60 second visual UAT can simply animate the conversation rather than trying to explain the entire architecture.
