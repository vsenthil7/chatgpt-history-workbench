## 06/10/2026 19:44 — Why I Can't Read YouTube Transcript

Because **what you see in the YouTube app/browser and what my web-access system can retrieve are different things**.

YouTube may have a transcript for that video, but the transcript often isn't included in the normal public HTML page that a web retrieval system receives. YouTube loads many things dynamically through its own internal services after the page opens—including captions/transcripts—and access can also depend on cookies, consent state, login, region, client type, and automated-access restrictions.

So there are **three separate capabilities**:

| Capability | From your link |
|---|---|
| Identify/open the YouTube page | ✅ |
| Read ordinary public page information | ✅ Usually |
| Play/listen to the audio like you do | ❌ Not through my web access |
| Retrieve YouTube's transcript | ❌ Not reliably |
| Analyse a transcript once I have it | ✅ |

Importantly, this **doesn't necessarily mean the video has no transcript**. You may open the same video in YouTube and see **Show transcript**, while my retrieval route doesn't receive that transcript.

And this isn't fundamentally an AI intelligence limitation. It's an **access/interface limitation**: if YouTube exposed the transcript to the interface available to me, I could analyse it directly.

### For your **10-video hardware course**

The human should ideally do **one action only**: provide the playlist URL.

The automation discovers the 10 video IDs and metadata, then attempts caption retrieval. If captions aren't available, a permitted audio/transcription route can be used. Each transcript should retain provenance such as:

`course → video → chapter → timestamp → transcript segment`

That's important. You don't want an LLM simply swallowing ten huge transcripts and producing a summary. You want it to know **which instructor said what, in which video, and at what timestamp**.

Then the AI can produce something much more valuable than ten summaries:

> “Video 2 introduced CPU cache. Video 5 depends on that concept when explaining memory latency. You understood L1/L2/L3 but your answers indicate you haven't understood cache misses. Before Video 6, I'll reteach that concept.”

That becomes a **learning system**, not a transcript system.

### Technologies

You don't need to invent every component. A practical stack could use the urlYouTube Data APIhttps://developers.google.com/youtube/v3 for playlist/video discovery and metadata. Transcript acquisition needs a separate strategy because YouTube's official captions API has authorization/ownership constraints; it isn't simply a public “give me captions for any video” API.

For videos you are authorized to process, speech-to-text can provide the fallback. After that, Python + an LLM + a vector/database layer can handle segmentation, concepts, retrieval, quizzes and learner state.

There is an important architectural distinction:

**YouTube is merely one source.**

Eventually:

```text

       ▼               ▼               ▼

       │               │               │
       └───────────────┼───────────────┘
                       ▼
              Knowledge Normalizer
                       ▼
                Learning Graph
                       ▼
               Personal AI Tutor
```

Then tomorrow the human could provide **a playlist + three PDFs + lecture notes**, and the same tutor could teach from all of them while retaining source provenance.

**For the use case you're describing, this is very feasible today.** The tricky part isn't the LLM. The first engineering problem to solve cleanly is **reliable, authorized acquisition of the video knowledge**, particularly when captions aren't publicly obtainable.
