# 11. What Claude missed

Four areas deserve explicit addition to the research agenda.

### Important now — relying-party model

TalkProof needs a formal answer to:

**Who relies on a certificate, for what decision, under what contractual/legal basis, and what exactly are they entitled to infer from it?**

Cryptography can prove integrity without proving that the underlying statement is true.

That distinction is fundamental to a "proof" product.

### Important now — trust-root governance

Who controls:

- signing keys;
- TSA relationships;
- reviewer authority;
- verifier releases;
- revocation;
- compromised certificates;
- erroneous certificates?

The technical evidence chain is strong, but organisational trust governance is not yet equally developed.

### ≤6–12 months — evidence correction/revocation

TalkProof needs a lifecycle for:

`valid certificate → later discovered error → correction/supersession/revocation → relying party can discover new status`

Long-term immutability without a strong correction model can preserve a mistake extremely well.

### ≤12 months — liability/economic boundary

If TalkProof becomes the independent evidence operator, that creates commercial questions:

- liability for incorrect certification;
- professional indemnity;
- SLA for verification longevity;
- what happens if TSA/anchor/provider disappears;
- cost of retaining/verifying evidence for years.

That may matter more commercially than several intelligence features.
