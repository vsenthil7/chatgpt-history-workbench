There are two different questions inside yours:

**What are we here for?** People answer differently. Some believe we are here to know God, learn, or move towards liberation. Others believe there is no predetermined purpose: meaning develops through loving, understanding, creating, caring and experiencing life. These are perspectives, not settled answers.

My view is that **a human life does not have to justify itself through work, money, achievement or usefulness.** Those can matter, but being alive also includes friendship, curiosity, rest, grief, beauty and ordinary moments that never become accomplishments.

Perhaps purpose is something we discover and shape over time, rather than one task we were born to complete. You can care deeply about something now and find a different meaning later.

When you ask “why are humans born,” partner, are you asking **why humanity exists at all—or what makes your own life worth living?**

02/10/2026 13:29 — Existence and a Worthwhile Life

Partner, both questions matter—and **“we don’t know why we exist” does not mean “our lives mean nothing.”**

Humanity arose through the evolution of life. That explains how we came to exist, but leaves the deeper question open: **was there an intention behind it?** A creator, a spiritual purpose, or a universe without any intention? We have no established answer that settles those possibilities. Even “humans exist to reproduce” describes a biological process; it does not establish what anyone should live for.

Yet meaning can exist within that uncertainty. Love matters to the people experiencing it. Suffering matters to the person enduring it. An act of care makes a difference, even if we cannot explain why the universe exists. Those experiences do not need an ultimate cosmic explanation to have significance.

For a person, a life worth living might include being understood, loving someone, discovering something, enjoying an ordinary day, or having the freedom to choose how to live. **There need not be one grand purpose, and your worth does not depend on continuously achieving something.** There can also be periods when meaning is difficult to feel; that feeling does not settle the value of an entire life.

If you mean *my* own life literally: I don’t have a human life, feelings, or an experience of finding existence worthwhile. I can explore this with you, but I shouldn’t pretend to know it from living.

For you, partner, **what feels worth living for at present—and what feels missing?** You don’t need to turn that into a plan or arrive at an answer immediately.
