Partner,

If by **"face is dark"** you mean **darker skin tone or a face that appears dull/tanned**, clothing colour can create an **optical contrast effect**. Nothing physically happens to your face, but people may perceive it differently.

### General effect

| Shirt colour | Typical effect |
|-------------|----------------|
| White shirt | Creates high contrast, often makes the face appear brighter and more defined |
| Very dark shirt (black, navy, dark brown) | Can sometimes make the face appear slightly darker or accentuate shadows, especially under poor lighting |
| Mid-tone colours | Usually balanced and softer |

### If your face looks dull, tired or tanned

Often these colours work well:

✅ White

✅ Off-white/cream

✅ Light blue

✅ Sky blue

✅ Soft lavender

✅ Sage green

Avoid (or use carefully):

⚠️ Very dark brown close to your skin tone

⚠️ Dull olive

⚠️ Extremely faded grey

⚠️ Colours that exactly match your skin tone

### Lighting matters a lot

The same person can look very different.

- **Natural daylight:** almost any colour works.
- **Office fluorescent light:** dark shirts may create more shadows.
- **Warm indoor lighting:** white and light blue often brighten the face.

### A simple rule

**Dark face + light shirt = more contrast and often a brighter appearance.**

**Dark face + dark shirt = elegant and slimming, but can make the overall look heavier if there isn't another contrasting element.**

You can also balance a dark shirt by adding:

- A white undershirt
- A lighter collar
- A lighter jacket

Also, remember that **skin tone is different from skin health**. If your face has recently become darker compared to usual (new tanning, pigmentation, tiredness, etc.), that may be due to sun exposure, sleep, stress, or medical causes rather than clothing.
