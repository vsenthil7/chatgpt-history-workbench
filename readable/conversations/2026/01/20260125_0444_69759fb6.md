Activity 1 - Icebreaker - Make up of (5 mins)

This activity makes you and the student comfortable with each other.

Instructions:

Tell an object to the student.
Example: Car
Ask the student to tell any three things that are used to make that object.
Example: Plastic, Paint, and Rubber.
Then, ask the student to tell an object to you.
You need to tell any three things that are used to make that object.
Keep the activity simple.
Play as many rounds as possible till the time permits.
Help the student wherever required.

Activity 2 - Guess the Object (20 mins)

This activity helps the student to learn to ask questions.

Instructions:

You think of an object.
Tell the student to ask “yes” or “no” questions to find the object.
Only 20 questions are allowed to be asked.
The answer can be guessed at any question number.
You should only respond with “yes” or “no” answers.
Any type of question counts.
If the student guesses the answer:
Correct - Appreciate their effort.
Wrong - Reveal the answer.
Now, ask the student to think of an object.
Then, you can ask questions to identify the object.
Play as many rounds as possible within the given time.
Help the student wherever required.

<aside> ❓ Example: Dog

Is it a living being? Yes.
Is it a human? Yes.
Is it commonly kept as a pet? Yes.
Is it known for its loyalty? Yes.
Is it often trained for various tasks? Yes.
Guess: Dog? Correct (6/20 questions). </aside>

Activity 3 - Analyse the Statement (15 mins)

This activity helps the student to practice to ask questions.

Instructions:

Explain the given 1 example to the student.
Tell a statement about yourself.
Ask the student to raise a question based on your statement to get more information.
Then, ask the student to share a statement about themselves.
You should raise a question related to the student's statement.
Examples are given below for your reference.
Take turns and repeat this till time permits.
Help your student wherever required.

Statement: I recently started learning to play the guitar.

## 🧊 Activity 1 – Icebreaker (5 mins)

### How to run it smoothly
- Start **very simple objects**
- Avoid abstract things

### Good starter objects (safe picks)
- Pen  
- Chair  
- Bottle  
- Shoe  
- Phone  

### Your role
- If the student struggles, **prompt gently**
  - “Think about what you can touch”
  - “What material is it made of?”

### Watch for:
- Overthinking → remind them *“no right or wrong”*
- Silence → give one example, then pause

✅ Success sign: student laughs or answers faster by round 2–3

---

## 🕵️ Activity 2 – Guess the Object (20 mins)

### This is the **core learning activity**

#### Before starting, clearly say:
> “You can only ask **yes or no questions**.  
> Any question counts. Guess anytime.”

### Pick **objects appropriate to age**
Avoid vague things like “idea”, “happiness”.

**Good object categories**
- Animals
- Household items
- School objects
- Food items

### If the student asks weak questions:
Gently model better ones *after* answering.

Example:
- Student: “Is it nice?”
- You: “Yes. But try asking something more specific.”

### When student guesses:
- Correct → **praise the process**, not intelligence  
  > “Nice questioning. You narrowed it well.”
- Wrong → Reveal calmly, no drama

### When roles reverse (you ask):
**Demonstrate good questioning**
- Category → properties → specifics

This is silent teaching.

---

## 🧠 Activity 3 – Analyse the Statement (15 mins)

### Explain this *very clearly* (important)
> “A statement gives information.  
> A question helps us understand more.”

### Start with **your statement**
Keep it simple and relatable.

**Good volunteer statements**
- “I like morning walks.”
- “I enjoy listening to music.”
- “I recently learned something new.”

If student struggles to ask:
- Offer **question starters**
  - Why…
  - How…
  - When…
  - Who…

### When student gives their statement:
- Ask **open-ended questions**
- Avoid yes/no here

Example:
- Student: “I like cricket.”
- You: “What do you like most about playing cricket?”

---

## 📌 Learning of the Day (5 mins)

Ask **exactly like this** (don’t lecture):
> “What did you learn today?”

If they say:
- “Nothing” → prompt:
  - “Did you learn anything about asking questions?”
- One-word answer → ask:
  - “Can you explain a little?”

You can *summarize back*:
> “So today you learned that good questions help us understand better.”
