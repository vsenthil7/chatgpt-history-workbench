Session 8 - Recognising the ‘Aha’ Moment

Activity 1 - Icebreaker - Buzz (5 mins)

This activity makes you and the student comfortable with each other.

Instructions:

Start the game by telling number one (1).
Then, the student tells number two (2).
You should tell buzz instead of number three (3).
You and the student tell ‘buzz’ for all the multiples of 3.
Continue to play in the same way till number 100.
If you or the student miss to tell buzz, start the game again from number 1.
Appreciate each other if you both reach number 100.
Help the student wherever required.

Activity 2 - Puzzles (20 mins)

This activity helps the student to experience the surprise moments.

Instructions:

Explain the “Aha moment” to the student.
A moment where you suddenly understand something, have a good idea, or find the answer to a problem.
Ask the given puzzles one at a time to the student.
After each puzzle, give the student 1 or 2 minutes to think and answer.
Provide hints if the student struggles to arrive at the given answer.
Share the correct answer, if required.
Help the student wherever required.

Puzzles

<aside> 1️⃣ A man lives on the 10th floor of a building. Every morning, he takes the elevator to the ground floor to go to work. Upon returning from work, he always goes up to floor 7th in the elevator. Then he uses the upstairs to the remaining 3 floors. Why?

Answer: The man is very short, so he has no problem pressing the button on the ground floor, but he does not reach the 10th floor button. The maximum he reaches is up to the 7th floor button.

</aside>

<aside> 2️⃣ Kumar dies of a heart attack at work. His colleagues are there when it happens. However, they do not call for help until Francis dies. Find why?

Answer: Kumar was a theatre actor and suffered a heart attack in the middle of a performance. Also, he was playing someone who was having a heart attack. Therefore, both his colleagues and the audience simply thought that he was giving a really impressive performance.

</aside>

<aside> 3️⃣ A black cat is walking through the middle of a road where there is a broken street lamp. A man is driving his vehicle with headlights that do not work. Despite all of this, he is able to avoid harming the cat.

Answer: Because all this happened in the daytime, and there was sunlight.

</aside>

<aside> 4️⃣ A man walks into a hotel and orders a glass of water. The waiter points a gun at him, but the man thanks the waiter and leaves. Why did the man thank the waiter?

Answer: The man had the hiccups. By scaring the man, the waiter cured him.

</aside>

<aside> 5️⃣ A car driver causes an accident when he crashes into a bike as he is making an unexpected turn at a crossroad. When the policemen arrive, another man gets arrested. The one who was driving is carried home. Why?

Answer: The driver was receiving driving lessons, and his instructor told him to make an incorrect turn at a crossroad and crash into a bike. The policemen considered the driving instructor to be responsible for the accident, so they arrested him and took the learner home.

</aside>

<aside> 6️⃣ A truck is stuck under a bridge, and the driver cannot get it out. A man walks by and stops to help. He easily gets the truck unstuck. How did he do it?

Answer: He let the air out of the tyres.

</aside>

Activity 3 - 10 Solutions (15 mins)

This activity brings the student to experience 'Aha!' moments when solving problems.

Instructions:

Ask the student:
Be ready with a pen and note/paper.
Note down their answers to the given problems.
Explain the given example to the student.
Tell the given scenarios one at a time to the student.
Give the student 1 or 2 minutes to think and write an answer for each scenario.
The student should write as many solutions as possible.
Encourage the student to think creatively and quickly.
Once the student is done answering, discuss their answers.
Appreciate and provide feedback.
Help the student wherever required.

<aside> ❓ Example Scenario: You have to attend college, but public transport is on strike.

Solutions:

I will find someone who has a bike and go with them.
I will ask my mother/father to drop me off.
I will attend an online class.
I will miss college and ask my friend to tell me what happened.
I will inform the college and see what they say. </aside>

<aside> 💡 Scenarios:

You own a hotel, but plastic is now banned. How will you handle it?
You are the college cultural secretory. Your chief guest for the program cancelled at the last minute. What will you do? </aside>
Learning of the Day

<aside> <img src="/icons/alarm_red.svg" alt="/icons/alarm_red.svg" width="40px" /> Ask the student to share their learning of the day.

Learnings:

Experiencing the ‘Aha’ moment after answering multiple solutions.
Each part of the question is important to give the correct answer. </aside>

Activity 4 - Homework of the Day (5 mins)

This activity encourages the student to learn and practice.

Instructions:

Discuss the previous session's homework.

Topic: What is Innovation?

Some pointers for your reference:

Innovation means thinking in new ways and having fresh ideas.
Innovation is making new things or improving old ones cleverly.
It is finding better ways to solve problems or do things.
It is inventing new stuff, making things better, or doing things differently.
Innovations make life easier, more fun and can change the world.

Explain the given homework to the student.

Encourage the student to complete their homework.

Give some examples or tips to the student.

Inform the student that homework will be discussed in the next session.

<aside> 📝 Research, Learn and Write 5 points about the below-given topic.

Topic: Should arts and crafts be part of the education system?

</aside>

**At-TE-202601-2-S08-12-CT-Session-8 – Recognising the ‘Aha’ Moment**

**Session Duration:** ~60 minutes  
**Core Intent:**  
Help the student *feel* the moment when the brain suddenly reframes a problem — not just solve it.

---

## 🎯 Session Spine (Big Picture)
This session is about **pattern-break → pause → surprise → clarity**.

If the student only gives “correct answers” but doesn’t *feel* surprise, you missed the goal.

---

## Activity 1 – Icebreaker: **Buzz** (5 mins)

### What you do
- Explain rules once, **then start immediately**.
- Keep tone playful, not exam-like.

### What you’re actually training
- **Inhibition control** (resisting saying the obvious number)
- **Rule switching**
- Early micro-failure → reset → resilience

### Mentor tip
When someone misses:
> “Nice. That’s exactly how thinking trips us.”

Do **not** scold. Restart cheerfully.

### Success marker
They reach 100 **with laughter**, not tension.

---

## Activity 2 – Puzzles: **Engineering the ‘Aha’** (20 mins)

### How to explain “Aha moment” (use simple words)
> “Sometimes your brain gets stuck in one direction.  
Suddenly — boom — you see it differently.  
That surprise feeling is called an *Aha moment*.”

Pause. Let that land.

---

### How to run EACH puzzle
1. Read puzzle slowly  
2. **Silence for 60–120 seconds** (don’t rescue early)  
3. Ask:
   - “What are you assuming?”
   - “What if one detail is different?”
4. Only then give hints.

⚠️ Do NOT rush to the answer. The *delay* creates the Aha.

---

### What each puzzle is secretly teaching

| Puzzle | Hidden Skill |
|------|-------------|
| Lift / 10th floor | Physical constraints ≠ logical rules |
| Theatre heart attack | Context matters more than action |
| Black cat | Time assumption bias |
| Gun & water | Emotional state as solution |
| Driving instructor | Responsibility ≠ actor |
| Truck under bridge | Reduce the problem, not force it |

If the student reacts with:
> “Ohhhh 😮”  
You’ve won.

---

## Activity 3 – **10 Solutions Game** (15 mins)

### This is the CORE Aha engine of the session

### Rule you must enforce
❌ No judging  
❌ No “right answer”  
✅ Quantity over quality

### Script you can use
> “Bad ideas welcome. Fast ideas win.”

---

### Scenario 1  
**Hotel + Plastic Ban**

If they’re stuck, nudge with categories:
- Materials
- Suppliers
- Customer behaviour
- Policy workaround
- Design change

Let them hit **5+ ideas**, even silly ones.

---

### Scenario 2  
**Chief Guest Cancels**

Push beyond “find another guest”:
- Change format
- Change audience role
- Change purpose of event
- Virtual presence
- Turn problem into story

### Where the Aha happens
When they realise:
> “There isn’t one correct solution.”

That’s the learning.

---

## Learning of the Day (5 mins)

### Ask exactly this:
> “What surprised you today?”

Not:
- “What did you learn?”
- “What was interesting?”

### Listen for phrases like:
- “I didn’t think of that”
- “I assumed…”
- “I suddenly understood…”

Reflect it back once. Don’t add commentary.

---

## Activity 4 – Homework (5 mins)

### Bridge from today → homework
Say:
> “Today you saw ideas coming from unexpected places.  
Arts and crafts work the same way.”

---

### Homework
**Topic:** *Should arts and crafts be part of the education system?*

### Give structure (optional, not mandatory)
Suggest they think in:
1. Learning
2. Brain development
3. Confidence
4. Problem-solving
5. Enjoyment

⚠️ Do NOT over-explain. Let them explore.

# 🧠 Palindrome – Concept Clear Version

ஒரு word அல்லது string:

- Last ல இருந்து read பண்ணினாலும்  

**Same spelling + same meaning**

- `madam`
- `mom`
- `level`
- `radar`

இது pattern symmetry.

Algorithm =  
👉 ஒரு problem solve பண்ண step-by-step instructions.

text = input("Enter a string: ")

## 🧩 `text[::-1]` என்ன?

இது தான் student confuse ஆன இடம்.

Python slicing format:

```
[start : stop : step]
```

`[::-1]` என்றால்:

- Start → beginning
- Stop → end
- Step → -1 (reverse direction)

So இது string-ஐ reverse பண்ணும்.

Example:

```
madam
```

Reverse:

```
madam
```
