- Add **4 cups water**.

## Why **milk is NOT used** to boil rice–dal for sweet pongal

### 1️⃣ Milk blocks starch release (core reason)
Milk contains:
- **Fat**
- **Proteins (casein, whey)**

When rice + dal boil in milk:
- Fat coats the grain surface (same problem as early ghee)
- Proteins bind with starch granules
- Result: **slower gelatinisation**

➡️ You get **separate grains / grainy body**, not the soft temple mash.

Temples want **maximum starch extraction first**.

---

### 2️⃣ Milk burns before rice is cooked
Milk:
- Scorches easily
- Forms skin
- Sticks at the bottom

In large pots:
- Bottom heat is intense
- Milk = guaranteed burning risk

So temples **never gamble** with milk early.

---

### 3️⃣ Milk + jaggery interaction is unstable
Jaggery is acidic and mineral-rich.

If milk is present **early**:
- Milk proteins can split slightly
- Flavour becomes dull
- Shelf life reduces

That’s why traditional recipes avoid milk until the very end — or skip it entirely.

---

### 4️⃣ Shelf life logic (important)
Temple pongal is often served over hours.

- Water + jaggery + ghee → stable
- Milk → spoils faster

So water is safer and more consistent.

---

## Then WHEN is milk allowed?
