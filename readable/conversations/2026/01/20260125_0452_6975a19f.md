Session 5 - Visualisation

</aside>

Activity 1 - Icebreaker - Attribute (5 mins)

This activity makes you and the student comfortable with each other.

Instructions:

Ask the student:
Name three vehicles that have four wheels.
Share their answer.
Example: Baby stroller, Skateboard, and Car.
After that, the student can ask a question like "Name three eatable items that are Red”.
You should try to answer it.
Ask for different attributes of three items.
Play as many rounds as possible till time permits.
Help the student wherever required.

Activity 2 - Visualise your Trip (20 mins)

This activity helps the student to learn to visualise.

Instructions:

Explain the below given “Steps to Visualise” to the student.
Ensure both of you are clear about the Steps to Visualise.
Now, you and the student take 5 minutes to:
Visualise your favourite trip in the past.
Then, share with each other in a detailed/expressive way.
Help the student wherever required.

Steps to Visualise

<aside> 1️⃣ Setting the Scene:

Close your eyes and take a deep breath to relax and focus.
Visualise the location of your favourite journey.
Picture the surroundings, the colours, the weather, and any landmarks. </aside>

<aside> 2️⃣ Engaging the Senses:

Recall and imagine the sounds that were present during the experience.
Recall the smells and tastes associated with the journey.
Was there a particular scent in the air or a memorable meal you had? </aside>

<aside> 3️⃣ Embracing Emotions:

Reflect on the emotions you felt during the journey.
Were you excited, peaceful, awe-struck, or maybe a combination of feelings?
Try to re-experience those emotions as you visualise them. </aside>

<aside> 4️⃣ Recalling Activities and Interactions:

Think about the activities you did and the interactions you had.
Were you exploring, resting, meeting new people, or learning something new? </aside>

<aside> 5️⃣ Highlighting Key Moments:

Identify a few key moments that stood out during the journey.
What made these moments memorable?
Visualise these moments in detail, capturing the essence of what made them special. </aside>

Activity 3 - Describe and Discover (15 mins)

This activity helps the student to visualise through verbal communication.

Instructions:

Ask the student:
Choose an object from the ones that you can see at the moment.
Describe an object chosen without telling its name.
Details, colours, and textures can be mentioned.
You should guess the object based on the student’s description.
Switch roles, and play as many rounds as possible till time permits.
Examples are given for your reference.
Help the student wherever required.

Examples:

<aside> 1️⃣ Object: Book

Description: It's a thin, rectangular item with many pages inside. The cover is hard and has a picture of a dragon on it. It is used for reading and is often found in libraries.

</aside>

<aside> 1️⃣ Object: Calculator

Description: It's a small, square device with a grid of buttons, each with a number or symbol. It is often used for calculations and has a small screen to display results.

</aside>

Learning of the Day

<aside> <img src="/icons/alarm_red.svg" alt="/icons/alarm_red.svg" width="40px" /> Ask the student to share their learning of the day.

Some learnings are given below for your reference.

Learnings:

Practising visualisation helps to remember and think creatively.
Visualisation helps to give clear information.
Use visualisation to guess the objects based on the description. </aside>

Activity 4 - Homework of the Day (5 mins)

This activity encourages the student to learn and practice.

Instructions:

Discuss the previous session's homework.

Topic: Imagination

Some pointers for your reference:

Imagination is like magic, creating new ideas and worlds.
You can imagine flying to the moon or talking to animals.
It is a fun game where you make stories or draw.
Imagination helps solve problems in new ways.
Everyone has an imagination.

Explain the given homework to the student.

Encourage the student to complete their homework.

Give some examples or tips to the student.

Inform the student that homework will be discussed in the next session.

</aside>

## 🧊 Activity 1 – Icebreaker: Attribute (5 mins)

### What you are *actually* training
- Categorisation
- Attribute switching
- Mental flexibility

### How to run it (script)
1. **You ask first** (keep it simple):
   > “Name three vehicles that have four wheels.”

2. **Accept imperfect answers**  
   Don’t correct too much — the goal is comfort, not accuracy.

3. Student asks back:
   > “Name three red eatable items.”

4. You answer **confidently**, even if silly.
5. Change attributes every round:
   - colour
   - size
   - use
   - place found
   - material

💡 *If student freezes*:  
Give **one example**, then let them continue.

---

## 🧠 Activity 2 – Visualise Your Trip (20 mins)

### This is the **heart of the session**

### Step 1: Explain the method (2–3 mins)
Keep it slow and reassuring:
> “There is no right or wrong visualisation. Whatever comes to your mind is correct.”

Walk them through the **5 steps** briefly:
1. Scene  
2. Senses  
3. Emotions  
4. Activities  
5. Key moments  

---

### Step 2: Silent visualisation (5 mins)
Say clearly:
> “Close your eyes. Take a deep breath. Imagine your favourite trip.”

⚠️ Don’t talk continuously.  
Silence is part of the exercise.

---

### Step 3: Sharing (10–12 mins)

#### Student shares first
Prompt gently:
- “Where was it?”
- “What could you see?”
- “What sounds were there?”
- “How did you feel?”

Let them speak **without interruption**.

#### Then you share
Model **rich detail**, but don’t dominate.

Example tone:
> “I remember the colour of the sky… the smell of food… the calm feeling…”

💡 *If student gives very short answers*:  
Ask **sensory prompts**, not “why” questions.

---

## 🗣️ Activity 3 – Describe & Discover (15 mins)

### Skill being trained
- Precise verbal description
- Listener’s imagination
- Attention to detail

### How to run it
1. Student chooses an object **visible right now**
2. Rules:
   - ❌ Don’t say the name
   - ✅ Describe colour, shape, use, texture

3. You guess.
4. Switch roles.

### If student struggles
Give scaffolding:
- “What colour is it?”
- “Is it soft or hard?”
- “What do people use it for?”

🎯 Don’t rush guesses — let description develop.

---

## 🪞 Learning of the Day (5 mins)

Ask **open reflection**:
> “What did you learn today?”

If they struggle, gently guide:
- “Was it easy or hard to imagine?”
- “Did describing help you remember better?”

⚠️ Do **not** lecture here.  
This is **their meaning-making moment**.

- Can be in **their own words**
