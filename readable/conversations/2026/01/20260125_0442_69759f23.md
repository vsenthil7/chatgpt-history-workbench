Activity 1 - Icebreaker - Boom or Busted (5 mins)

This activity makes you and the student comfortable with each other.

Instructions:

Tell the name of an object; it can be a living or a non-living thing.
On hearing the word, if it is a living thing, the student should say “Boom.”
Example: If you say mouse, the student should say "Boom."
On hearing the word, if it is a non-living thing, the student should say “Busted.”
Example: If you say paper, the student should say "Busted."
Next, ask the student to give you a living or non-living thing.
Now, you can tell "Boom" or "Busted.”
Take turns and play this till time permits.
Tell the words quickly to make the game interesting and fun.
Help the student wherever required.

Activity 2 - Answer the strange questions (20 mins)

This activity helps the student to improve observation skills.

Instructions:

Ask the questions given below to the student one at a time.
Ask the student to share the answer.
Appreciate their effort for the correct answer.
Share the correct answer if they are wrong.
Feel free to ask your own questions.
Help the student wherever required.

Questions:

If you had a machine that could generate one crore rupees a day, how much would you be willing to pay for it?

Answer: Why pay for the machine if you already have it?

What weighs more - 100 pounds of feathers, or 100 pounds of quarters?

Answer: They weigh the same.

A little girl kicks a soccer ball. It goes 10 feet and comes back to her. How is this possible?

Answer: She kicked it straight up into the air.

If it took 8 men, 10 hours to build a wall, how long would it take 4 men to build the same wall?

Answer: The wall is already built; no need for anyone to build it again.

Your friend says he can predict the exact score of every football game before it begins. He is right every time. How is that possible?

Answer: The score before any football game begins is 0:0.

In South Africa, you can not take a picture of a man with a wooden leg. Why not?

Answer: You can not take a picture with a wooden leg; you have to use a camera.

Can you name three consecutive days without using the words Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, or Saturday?

Answer: Yesterday, Today, Tomorrow.

What goes up and down but never moves?

Answer: A staircase.

A cowboy rides into town on Friday, stays for three days, and leaves on Friday. How is that possible?

Answer: His horse's name is Friday.

If there are three apples and you take away two, how many apples do you have?

Answer: You have two apples because you took them.

Activity 3 - Listen and repeat the story with a change (15 mins)

This activity helps the student to practise observation skills.

Instructions:

Tell the below story or share any story of your choice.
Ask the student to listen carefully.
Once done, ask the student to repeat the story and twist it in between or at the end.
A sample answer is given for your reference.
Help the student if required.

Story:

One day, a lady went to her kitchen for a snack. When she opened her refrigerator, she saw a mouse running across the floor. She was frightened of mice, so she screamed and stood on a chair. The mouse was frightened by the lady’s scream, so it ran away. So, the lady climbed down from the chair, took her purse, and went to the store to buy a mousetrap. The man at the store gave her a mousetrap and showed her how it worked. He told her to put a piece of cheese in the trap. When she got home, she realized that she did not have any cheese in her refrigerator. So she cut out a picture of a piece of cheese from a magazine. After she set the trap, she turned off the light and went to bed. The next morning, she saw that the mousetrap had worked! Inside the trap, next to the picture of the piece of cheese, was a picture of a mouse!

Sample Answer - Story with a twist:

She went to a bookstore and bought a book on ‘How to Catch a Mouse’. She started reading and reading and fell asleep in the middle. The next day, she got a mouse trap as it was suggested in the book and set it up. Soon, the mouse was caught in the trap. When she went to see the mouse, she saw that it was in a lot of pain. The mouse asked the lady, 'Dear Lady, you were afraid of me, and I was afraid of you. But I left the scene and did not cause you harm, but you went and got a trap for me. Is it good?' The woman woke up with a shock, realised it was a dream, and decided not to set up a trap for the mouse.

### **At-TE-202601-2-S02-06-CT-Session 2 – Observation Skills**

**Total Duration:** ~60 minutes  
**Student Level:** Any (adjust pace, not content)  
**Core Skill Focus:** Observation → Listening → Perspective shift

---

## **Session Objective (Big Picture)**
By the end of this session, the student should:
- Pause before answering  
- Listen carefully instead of reacting fast  
- Notice *hidden assumptions* in questions and stories  

Observation ≠ seeing fast  
Observation = **seeing correctly**

---

## **Activity 1 – Icebreaker: Boom or Busted (5 mins)**

### **Purpose**
- Warm up the brain  
- Train **quick categorisation**  
- Remove hesitation and fear of “wrong answers”

### **How to Run**
1. Say an object name (living or non-living)
2. Student responds:
   - **Living → “Boom”**
   - **Non-living → “Busted”**
3. Switch roles
4. Speed it up gradually

### **Facilitator Tips**
- Start slow → then rapid fire  
- Laugh at mistakes (important for comfort)  
- If student freezes, repeat rule once and continue

✅ **Do not correct harshly**  
✅ **Energy > accuracy**

---

## **Activity 2 – Answer the Strange Questions (20 mins)**

### **Purpose**
- Break “surface thinking”
- Teach the student to **re-read the question**
- Spot tricks, assumptions, and hidden frames

### **Flow for EACH Question**
1. Ask the question  
2. Let student answer fully (no interruption)  
3. Appreciate effort  
4. Reveal correct answer  
5. Ask:  
   > “What did you miss in the question?”

---

### **Questions & Anchors**

| Question Type | Observation Skill Triggered |
|---|---|
| One crore machine | Questioning assumptions |
| Feathers vs quarters | Units vs intuition |
| Ball comes back | Visualising direction |
| 8 men / wall | Time vs state |
| Football score | Before vs after |
| Wooden leg photo | Literal interpretation |
| 3 consecutive days | Language framing |
| Goes up & down | Function vs movement |
| Cowboy Friday | Names vs days |
| Apples taken | Ownership logic |

🧠 **Key Line to Reinforce:**  
> “Smart thinking starts with *reading carefully*, not answering quickly.”

---

## **Activity 3 – Listen & Repeat with a Twist (15 mins)**

### **Purpose**
- Deep listening  
- Memory recall  
- Creative alteration without breaking logic

### **Steps**
1. Read the story **slowly** (no rushing)
2. Ask student to retell:
   - Either twist the middle  
   - Or change the ending
3. Help only if they’re stuck

### **What to Observe as Facilitator**
- Did they keep core characters?
- Did cause → effect still make sense?
- Did they listen or invent randomly?

❌ Don’t judge creativity  
✅ Encourage *logical imagination*

---

## **Learning of the Day – Reflection (5 mins)**

Ask the student:
> **“What did you learn today?”**

Let them speak first.

If needed, gently nudge using:
- Thinking differently  
- Listening carefully  
- Seeing from another angle  
- Empathy in stories  

📌 **Do NOT lecture here. This is reflection, not teaching.**

---

## **Activity 4 – Homework Discussion & Assignment (5 mins)**

### **Review Previous Homework**
**Topic:** *What is a Mind Map?*

Reinforce simply:
- Central idea in the middle  
- Branches = related thoughts  
- Colours & pictures help memory  

(1–2 minutes max)
