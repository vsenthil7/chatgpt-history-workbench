## 1) EU Moves Closer to Enforceable AI Liability Regime

### What this means

This is saying Europe is moving from **“AI should be responsible”** to **“specific people and companies can be held accountable for specific failures.”**

That is a major shift.

Earlier AI governance discussions were often about:
- ethics
- principles
- voluntary commitments
- best practices

Now the EU framework is increasingly about:
- **legal obligations**
- **named responsibility**
- **documentation**
- **logs**
- **oversight**
- **penalties**
- **proof of compliance**

The EU AI Act already assigns obligations to different actors such as **providers** and **deployers**, and the implementation timeline continues to bring those obligations closer to real enforcement. Providers of general-purpose AI models have compliance pathways under the GPAI Code of Practice, while deployers of high-risk AI systems have concrete duties like oversight, logging, and notifying providers/authorities when risks emerge. citeturn973557search1turn973557search3turn973557search6turn973557search11

### Who is involved

There are several layers of actors:

#### a) **Model providers**
These are the companies building or releasing the model itself.  
Examples in the ecosystem generally include labs such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Mistral, Meta.

Their responsibility is closer to:
- model documentation
- safety measures
- transparency
- copyright-related compliance
- systemic-risk management for the most advanced models

The EU’s GPAI Code of Practice is specifically meant to help these providers comply with AI Act obligations on transparency, copyright, safety, and security. citeturn973557search1turn973557search8

#### b) **Deployers**
These are companies or institutions actually using the AI system in the real world:
- banks
- hospitals
- HR systems
- insurers
- governments
- employers

#### c) **Downstream users / integrators**
These are firms that:
- fine-tune models
- wrap models in products
- integrate them into workflows
- resell AI-powered tools
- combine multiple models and tools into one system

This matters because responsibility is no longer easily avoided by saying:

- high-risk systems
- transparency obligations
- general-purpose AI obligations
