This is your **core sharp, spicy chutney base** — fast, raw, and powerful. From this one base, you can later branch into multiple versions (like we did earlier).

---

# 🧩 1. Base Version (Raw – No Cooking)

### 🥣 Ingredients
- Green chillies – 8 to 12 (adjust spice level)
- Garlic – 3 to 5 cloves  
- Salt – to taste  
- Tamarind – small piece *(or ½ tsp lemon juice)*  
- Oil – 1 tsp (optional, smoothness)  

👉 Optional flavor boosters:
- Small onion / shallots – 2–3  
- Few curry leaves  
- Little coriander  

---

### ⚙️ Method (2–3 mins)
1. Roughly chop chillies  
2. Add everything into mixer  
3. Grind coarse or smooth (your choice)  
4. Add a few drops of water if needed  

✔️ Done. Sharp, spicy, raw chutney ready.

---

# ⚡ What This Base Gives You
- **Heat:** direct chilli punch  
- **Sharpness:** garlic + tamarind  
- **Freshness:** raw grind (no dulling from cooking)  

This is your **“high-energy chutney”** — best for:
- Idli / dosa  
- Curd rice  
- Plain rice + ghee  

---

# 🔄 2. Convert This Base → 3 Variants

## 🥜 A. Peanut Version (Body + Balance)
Add:
- Roasted peanuts – 2 tbsp  

👉 Result:
- Reduces sharp spice  
- Adds thickness + protein  
- More “hotel-style” feel  

---

## 🥥 B. Coconut Version (Cooling + Fresh)
Add:
- Fresh coconut – 2 tbsp  

👉 Result:
- Softens heat  
- Gives smooth texture  
- More South Indian breakfast style  

---

## 🥛 C. Curd Version (Cooling + Tangy)
Add:
- Thick curd – 2–3 tbsp  

👉 Result:
- Cuts spice strongly  
- Creamy + refreshing  
- Best for hot days  

---

# 🔥 Optional Tempering (Upgrade Layer)

If you want depth:

- Oil  
- Mustard seeds  
- Urad dal  
- Curry leaves  

Pour on top → transforms raw chutney into **restaurant-style finish**

| Too sharp | Add peanuts / oil |
| Too thick | Add little water / curd |

## 🌶️🔥 Oil-Cooked Green Chilli Chutney (Deep, Roasted Version)

This is the **next level from raw chutney** —  
less harsh, more **deep, roasted, and stable** (stores longer).

---

# 🧩 1. Core Cooked Version

### 🥣 Ingredients
- Green chillies – 8–10  
- Garlic – 5 cloves  
- Small onion / shallots – 5–6  
- Tamarind – small piece *(or 1 tsp)*  
- Salt – to taste  
- Oil – 2 to 3 tbsp *(important)*  

👉 Optional:
- Curry leaves  
- Few coriander stems  

---

### ⚙️ Method (Key = Proper Frying)

1. **Heat oil** in pan  
2. Add:
   - Garlic  
   - Onion  
   → sauté till light golden  

3. Add green chillies  
   → fry till:
   - Skin blisters  
   - Raw smell reduces  

4. Add tamarind + curry leaves  
   → cook 1–2 mins  

5. Cool completely  

6. Grind (no or very little water)

---

✔️ Final texture:
- Thick  
- Slightly oily  
- Deep green to olive color  

---

# 🔥 What Changes vs Raw Version

| Aspect | Raw Chutney | Oil-Cooked Chutney |
|-------|------------|--------------------|
| Taste | Sharp, aggressive | Rounded, deep |
| Spice feel | Direct hit | Slower, smoother |
| Shelf life | 1–2 days | 3–5 days |
| Aroma | Fresh | Roasted, rich |

👉 Oil cooking = **stability + flavor depth**

---

# ⚡ 2. Upgrade: Tempering Finish

After grinding, you can add:

- Oil  
- Mustard seeds  
- Urad dal  
- Curry leaves  

Pour on top → gives **hotel-style aroma + crunch**

---

# 🔄 3. Convert This Cooked Base

## 🥜 Peanut Version (Rich + Thick)
Add while grinding:
- 2–3 tbsp roasted peanuts  

👉 Result:
- Creamy  
- Less spicy  
- Good for dosa / idli  

---

## 🥥 Coconut Version (Balanced)
Add:
- 2 tbsp coconut  

👉 Result:
- Softer taste  
- Breakfast-friendly  

---

## 🧄 Extra Garlic Version (Bold)
Add:
- 2 more raw garlic cloves while grinding  

👉 Result:
- Punchy + strong (meals with rice)

---

# ⚖️ Control System

| Problem | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Too spicy | Add peanuts / coconut |
| Too oily | Add little hot water while grinding |
| Too thick | Add warm water (not cold) |
| Bitter taste | Over-fried → next time reduce frying |

---

# 🧠 Core Insight

Cooking in oil does 3 things:

> 🔥 Removes raw harshness  
> 🧠 Binds flavors together  
> 🛢️ Preserves chutney longer  

This is why **most “hotel chutneys” are oil-cooked first**.

---

## 🚀 When to Use This Version
- Dosa / Idli (best match)  
- Pongal  
- Rice + ghee  
- Travel food (longer shelf life)
