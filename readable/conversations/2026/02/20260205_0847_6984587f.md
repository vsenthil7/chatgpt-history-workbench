- Turmeric – pinch  
- Coriander powder – ½ tsp  
- Jeera powder – ¼ tsp

- Garam masala – *just a pinch*

- Roughly **2–2¼ cups water per 1 cup rava**

### 6️⃣ Add roasted rava (critical step)

- Add rava **slowly**, stirring continuously

Mix well → cover → cook on **low flame 3–4 mins**.

---

- Switch off
- Add lemon juice (to taste)
- Add chopped coriander leaves
- Fluff gently

Rest **2 minutes**, then serve.

## Ingredients (2 servings)

### Base
- Roasted semiya — **1 cup**
- Water — **2 cups** (see texture notes)
- Ghee — **2 to 2½ tbsp**
- Salt — as needed

### Tempering + aroma
- Mustard seeds — **½ tsp**
- Chana dal — **1 tsp**
- Curry leaves — **8–12 leaves**
- Green chilli — **1–2** (slit)

### Body (balanced, not heavy)
- Onion — **1 medium**, finely chopped
- Tomato — **1 small**, finely chopped
- Ginger–garlic paste — **½ tsp**

### Spices (kept clean)
- **Turmeric powder — ¼ tsp (must)**
- Coriander powder — **½ tsp**
- Jeera powder — **¼ tsp**
- Red chilli powder — **¼–½ tsp** (to taste)

### Finish (must)
- **Lemon juice — 1 to 2 tsp (must)**
- Coriander leaves — **2 tbsp** chopped

**Optional upgrade (if you ever have):** cashew nuts (10–12) + a pinch of hing.

---

## Step-by-step (with “why” so you never fail)

### Step 0 — Prep (30 seconds)
Chop onion + tomato. Slit chilli. Keep lemon ready for end.  
**Why:** Upma is fast; if you chop later, things burn.

---

### Step 1 — Tempering in ghee (foundation of taste)
1. Heat a kadai on **medium-low**.
2. Add **ghee**.
3. Add **mustard seeds** → wait till they splutter fully.
4. Add **chana dal** → fry till **golden**.
5. Add **curry leaves** + **green chilli**.

**Why this order:**  
Mustard needs hot fat; chana dal needs time to crisp; curry leaves/chilli release aroma at the end without burning.

---

### Step 2 — Onion (natural sweetness)
Add onion and sauté till **soft + translucent**, slight golden edges ok.

**Why:** Onion sweetness balances chilli + lemon later.

---

### Step 3 — Ginger–garlic paste (remove raw smell)
Add **½ tsp** paste and cook till the **raw smell is gone**.

**Why:** If this stays raw, the whole upma tastes sharp/uncooked.

---

### Step 4 — Tomato (makes it moist, not dry)
Add tomato + a pinch of salt. Cook till tomato becomes **fully mushy**.

**Why:** Tomato gives body so semiya feels “coated”, not plain.

---

### Step 5 — Spice bloom (don’t burn)
Lower flame. Add:
- **turmeric**
- coriander powder
- jeera powder
- chilli powder

Stir **20–30 seconds** only.

**Why:** Spices must “wake up” in ghee, but long cooking makes them bitter.

---

### Step 6 — Water + correct salt (this decides final taste)
Add **2 cups water** and remaining salt. Bring to a **rolling boil**.

Now taste the water:
- It should taste **slightly more salty than you want**
(because semiya will absorb it).

**Why:** If water is under-salted, finished upma will be bland and you can’t fix it properly later.

---

### Step 7 — Add semiya (no lumps, perfect texture)
Lower flame.
Add semiya **slowly in a steady rain** while stirring continuously.

Mix once well → cover → cook on low **2–3 minutes**.

**Why:** Slow add prevents lumps; low cook prevents sticking.

---

### Step 8 — Rest + finish (the “forever” signature)
Switch off. Keep covered **2 minutes** (resting finishes cooking).

Open, fluff gently.
Add:
- **lemon juice**
- coriander leaves  
(Optional: ½ tsp ghee on top)

**Why lemon at end:** Lemon turns bitter if boiled. Adding at end keeps it bright and fresh.

---

## Texture control (simple rule)
- **Fluffy/separate:** 1 cup semiya : **1¾ cups water**
- **Soft/comfort:** 1 cup semiya : **2 cups water**
- **Very soft (kids/elderly):** 1 cup semiya : **2¼ cups water**

---

## Emergency fixes
- **Sticky / too thick:** sprinkle hot water + cover 1 minute, fluff.
- **Too watery:** cook uncovered on low 1–2 min, stir gently.
- **Bland:** add salt + a squeeze of lemon + ½ tsp ghee (best rescue combo).
