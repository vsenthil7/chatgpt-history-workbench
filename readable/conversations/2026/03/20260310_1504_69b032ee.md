# Activity 1 – Icebreaker  
### Daily Delegation (5 min)

### Question to Student
> Share **5 tasks you ask others to do in your daily life.**

Example given in curriculum:
> I ask my mom to fill my water bottle.

### What you should observe

This activity reveals **how the student perceives delegation**.

| Possible Student Response | Meaning |
|---|---|
Cannot think of examples | Does not recognise delegation |
Only says family tasks | Limited leadership exposure |
Mentions friends/team tasks | Social delegation awareness |
Mentions organising tasks | Leadership thinking emerging |

### How to guide if student struggles

You can prompt:

Examples:

- Asking friend to bring notes
- Asking teammate to prepare slides
- Asking sibling to help with something
- Asking someone to collect information
- Asking someone to buy something

Goal:  
Student should realise **delegation already happens in daily life**.

---

# Activity 2 – Plan and Delegate  
### Solar System Presentation Scenario (20 min)

This is the **core learning activity**.

You are teaching the **4-step delegation model**.

---

# Step 1 — List All Tasks

Ask student:

> If your team must create a Solar System presentation,  
> what tasks must be completed?

Let the student think first.

Possible tasks:

- Research planets
- Find pictures
- Find videos
- Write explanation
- Prepare slides
- Decide speaking order
- Practice presentation

If student gets stuck, help slowly.

Do **not give answers immediately**.

---

# Step 2 — Identify Similar Tasks

Ask:

> Which tasks are similar?

Example grouping student may discover:

Research tasks  
Presentation tasks  
Practice tasks

This teaches **pattern recognition**.

---

# Step 3 — Categorise Tasks

Guide student to group tasks.

Example structure:

| Category | Tasks |
|---|---|
Research | Planet research + collect images |
Presentation | Slides + speaking points |
Preparation | Practice + rehearsal |

This step teaches **organisation thinking**.

---

# Step 4 — Delegate to Members

Ask:

> If your team has **5 members**, how will you distribute the work?

Example:

| Member | Task |
|---|---|
Member 1 | Mercury + Venus research |
Member 2 | Earth + Mars |
Member 3 | Jupiter + Saturn |
Member 4 | Uranus + Neptune |
Member 5 | Slides + presentation structure |

Then ask:

> What will **everyone do together?**

Expected answer:

- Practice
- Review
- Rehearsal

This step teaches **collaborative responsibility**.

---

# Activity 2 – Reflection Questions

Ask these slowly.

### 1️⃣ What steps did you learn?

Expected answer:

1. List tasks  
2. Identify similar tasks  
3. Categorise tasks  
4. Delegate tasks

---

### 2️⃣ Which step was hardest?

Important observation.

| Answer | Meaning |
|---|---|
Listing tasks hard | Planning skill weak |
Grouping tasks hard | Pattern recognition weak |
Delegation hard | Leadership confidence low |

---

### 3️⃣ Which step did you like?

Shows **student's thinking preference**.

---

### 4️⃣ How did you ensure fair work distribution?

Look for answers like:

- equal work
- based on strengths
- based on difficulty

This indicates **leadership maturity**.

---

### 5️⃣ How can delegation be improved?

Open thinking question.

---

### 6️⃣ Did you enjoy the activity?

Observe enthusiasm.

---

Guide student to divide into teams.

Example:

| Team | Responsibility |
|---|---|

Possible:

- 5 teams

### Step 4 — Oversight Structure

Ask student:

> How will you ensure everything runs smoothly?

Expected ideas:

- team leaders report to president
- planning meetings
- rehearsal day

This reveals **leadership architecture thinking**.
