# Session 5 - Evaluating Consequences and Making Decisions

<aside>
<img src="/icons/cursor-button_gray.svg" alt="/icons/cursor-button_gray.svg" width="40px" /> **Click on the inverted triangles below to read the instructions.**

</aside>

- **Activity 1 - Icebreaker - Would you Rather? (5 mins)**
    
    *This activity makes you and the student comfortable with each other.*
    
    **Instructions:**
    
    1. Share the given questions one at a time with the student.
    2. Ask the student to share their answer, followed by your answer.
        1. Share the answer with a reason.
    3. Help the student wherever required.
    
    <aside>
    🔰 **Questions:**
    
    1. Would you rather fly or be able to breathe underwater?
    2. Would you rather be 20 feet tall or 1 inch tall?
    3. Would you rather sing everything you say or shout everything you say?
    4. Would you rather be extremely smart but no one listens to you or average but lots of people listen to you?
    5. Would you rather always get up early or always sleep late?
    </aside>
    
- **Activity 2 - Evaluating Consequences and Making Decisions (20 mins)**
    
    *This activity helps the student evaluate the consequences of their decisions.*
    
    **Instructions:**
    
    1. Ask the student to be ready with a pen and note/paper.
    2. Explain the given meaning of “Dilemma” with example to the student.
    3. Tell the given list of Dilemmas one at a time to the student.
    4. Ask the student to list the possible consequences of both decisions for each scenario.
        1. Then ask them to pick one decision and explain the reason.
    5. Give 2 minutes to the student to think about each scenario.
    6. Finish as many dilemmas as possible in the given time.
    7. Encourage the student to think creatively.
    8. Once the student is done analysing and answering, discuss their answers.
        1. Provide feedback about the student's analysis.
    9. Help the student wherever required.
    
    <aside>
    ↔️ **Dilemma Meaning:** A situation when you have to make a difficult choice between two or more things.
    
    </aside>
    
    <aside>
    🗯️ **Example:** You see a classmate cheating on an exam, would you report it or not?
    
    1. **Report: (Choice)
    Consequences**
        1. Your classmate is no longer on good terms with you.
        2. Some of your friends are worried that you are not loyal.
        3. Some of your friends respect you more.
        4. Your teacher respects you more.
    2. **Don't report: (Choice)
    Consequences**
        1. Your classmate respects you more.
        2. Some of your friends respect you more.
        3. Some of your friends are disappointed with you.
        4. You are disappointed in yourself.
    </aside>
    
    <aside>
    🔀 **List of Dilemmas:**
    
    1. You see your friend being bullied, would you interfere or stay silent?
    2. You find some money on the floor, but it is not yours, would you keep it or give it to an authority figure?
    3. You see your sibling lying to your parents about something important, would you tell your parents or not?
    4. Your friends are pressuring you to do something you should not be doing, would you do it or not?
    5. If you see a friend harming the environment, would you confront them or let them go?
    </aside>
    
    <aside>
    📖 **Summary of the Activity:**
    
    <aside>
    🛑 **Note:**
    
    1. To summarise the activity:
        1. Ask the given questions to the student.

    3. Possible answers are given for your reference.
    4. Help the student wherever required.

    **Questions:**
    
    1. **Why are dilemmas tough to solve?**
        
        **Possible Answer:** I have to choose between what is right and what is easy.
        
    2. **What personal values or principles did you consider when making decisions in these dilemmas?**
        
        **Possible Answer:** I considered honesty, loyalty, empathy, and the well-being of others. These values guided my decision-making process.
        
    3. **How do you think your decisions in these dilemmas might impact your relationships with others involved?**
        
        **Possible Answer:** My decisions may strengthen some relationships while creating tension in others. It is a delicate balance, and I need to consider the consequences of my connections with others.
        
    4. **How might you apply the skills you developed in handling dilemmas to real-life situations?**
        
        **Possible Answer:** The ability to analyse consequences, consider values, and make tough decisions is valuable in real-life situations. I can apply these skills to navigate challenges and dilemmas with more confidence.
        
    5. **Reflecting on the dilemmas, are there any situations where the 'right' decision was not specific? Why or why not?**
        
        **Possible Answer:** Yes, in some dilemmas, both choices had valid reasons, and there was not a specific right or wrong. It made decision-making challenging.
        
    6. **Did you enjoy the activity?**
        
        **Open Answer:** Encourage the student to share their experience.
        
    </aside>

# Session-5 Execution Guide (Practical Flow)

1. Icebreaker – 5 min

4. Homework + Wrap – 5 min

# 1️⃣ Activity 1 – Icebreaker (5 min)

Purpose:  
Warm up his **thinking + speaking comfort**.

Since this student **takes time before answering**, do this differently.

### Ask → Give him 10 seconds → then answer yourself

Example:

**Q1:**  
Would you rather fly or breathe underwater?

Ask him:

> “If you could choose only one power — flying or breathing underwater — which one would you pick?”

If he pauses:

> “Take 10 seconds and imagine.”

Then let him answer.

Then you answer:

Example:

> “I would choose flying. Because I can travel fast, see the world, and help people during emergencies.”

---

### Ask only 3 questions (not all 5)

Better for slower thinkers.

Suggested:

1️⃣ Fly vs breathe underwater  
2️⃣ 20 feet tall vs 1 inch tall  
3️⃣ Extremely smart but no one listens vs average but people listen

The **third question is very powerful** for leadership thinking.

If he says:

> “Smart but no one listens”

You can ask:

> “Is intelligence useful if nobody listens?”

This triggers **leadership thinking**.

---

# 2️⃣ Activity 2 – Dilemma Thinking (20 min)

This is the **core learning activity**.

First explain simply.

### Explain Dilemma

Say:

> “A dilemma is when you have two choices, but both have consequences.”

Example:

> “Imagine you see your friend cheating in an exam.  
You must choose: report or stay silent.”

---

## How to Make Him Think (Important)

Instead of asking directly:

**Use 3-step thinking**

1️⃣ Choice  
2️⃣ Consequence  
3️⃣ Decision

---

## Dilemma 1

**Friend is bullied**

Ask:

> “If your friend is bullied, what are two choices?”

Let him answer.

If stuck, guide:

Choice A → Interfere  
Choice B → Stay silent

Then ask:

> “What could happen if you interfere?”

Possible consequences:

• bully may stop  
• bully may target you  
• friend feels supported  
• teacher may notice

Then:

> “What if you stay silent?”

• bullying continues  
• friend feels alone  
• you feel guilty  
• no conflict for you

Then ask:

> “Which decision would you choose?”

Important follow-up:

> “Why?”

---

## Dilemma 2

**Money on floor**

Ask:

> “If you find money in school, what are the two choices?”

Choice A  
Keep it

Choice B  
Give to teacher

Then consequences.

Guide thinking:

**Keep money**

• you gain money  
• owner loses  
• you may feel guilty

**Give teacher**

• teacher trusts you  
• owner may get it back  
• you lose money

---

## Dilemma 3 (Very important)

**Friends pressuring you**

Ask:

> “If friends pressure you to do something wrong, what are two choices?”

Choice A → join them  
Choice B → refuse

Consequences:

Join

• friends accept you  
• may get in trouble  
• bad habits

Refuse

• friends may tease  
• self-respect increases  
• safer decision

---

### Important mentoring trick

Ask:

> “Which decision gives **short-term benefit**?”

Then:

> “Which decision gives **long-term benefit**?”

This introduces **strategic thinking**.

---

# 3️⃣ Activity 3 – Hot Air Balloon (15 min)

This is a **moral reasoning test**.

Explain slowly.

Say:

> “There are 9 people stuck in a lift. Only one can be saved.”

Then show the list.

Doctor  
Politician  
Student  
Grandmother  
Priest  
Scientist  
Pregnant woman  
Friend’s brother  
Actor

Ask:

> “Who would you save?”

Then ask:

> “Why?”

---

## What to Observe (Important for your mentoring analysis)

You should observe **his decision logic**.

Students usually choose based on:

| Thinking Type | Choice |
|---|---|
| Emotional | friend’s brother |
| Social value | doctor |
| Future value | student |
| Humanity | pregnant woman |
| Knowledge value | scientist |

This activity reveals **moral framework**.

---

### Your answer (after he answers)

You can say:

> “Different people may choose differently.”

Example reasoning:

Doctor → can save many lives  
Scientist → may create inventions  
Pregnant woman → two lives  
Student → future potential

Important message:

> “Leadership decisions often require evaluating consequences.”

---

# 4️⃣ Homework (5 min)

Explain simply.

Say:

> “Find two news stories.”

Example:

Road accident  
New government policy  
Sports news  
Technology news

Then:

> “Write 3 consequences for different people.”

Example:

News: petrol price increase

Consequences:

You → travel cost increases  
Family → monthly expenses increase  
Public → transport price increase

---

# 5️⃣ End of Session Questions

Ask quickly:

1️⃣ Why are dilemmas difficult?

Expected thinking:

> “Because both choices have consequences.”

---

2️⃣ What values did you think about?

Guide words:

Honesty  
Loyalty  
Empathy  
Responsibility

---

3️⃣ Did you enjoy the activity?

Important for **engagement measurement**.

---

# 🎯 What This Session Actually Trains

This session builds **three cognitive skills**:

1️⃣ **Consequence thinking**

Before acting → think about outcomes.

---

2️⃣ **Ethical reasoning**

Right vs easy.

---

3️⃣ **Decision justification**

Explaining **why you chose something**.

---

# 🧠 One Subtle Skill You Should Observe Today

Based on **Sessions 1-4 pattern**.

Observe:

> Does he analyse consequences slowly but logically?

If yes → he may have **analytical decision ability**.

These students often succeed in:

• programming  
• system design  
• engineering  
• research roles

But struggle in:

• debate  
• sales  
• spontaneous speaking
