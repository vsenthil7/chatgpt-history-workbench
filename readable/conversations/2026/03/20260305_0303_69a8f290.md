# Session 2 – Mentoring Flow (Simple Guide)

## 1️⃣ Icebreaker – “I Couldn't Disagree More” (5 minutes)

Purpose:  
Make the student **comfortable speaking and reasoning**.

### Start like this

You can say:

> Today we will talk about **leadership**.  
> But first let’s start with a fun activity.  
> I will say a statement.  
> Your job is to **disagree with it and give a reason.**

---

### Statement 1
**“It is always good to be famous.”**

Ask:
> Why might being famous NOT be good?

Possible student answers:
- No privacy
- Too much pressure
- People judge you

If stuck, guide:

> Imagine you cannot go to the shop normally because everyone recognizes you.

---

### Statement 2
**“Homework is always helpful.”**

Ask:
> Can homework sometimes be bad?

Possible answers:
- Too much stress
- Less time for hobbies
- Students get tired

---

### Statement 3
**“Having a lot of money is the key to happiness.”**

Ask:
> Can someone be happy without a lot of money?

Possible answers:
- Family
- Friends
- Good health

You can add:

> Many people with a lot of money still feel lonely.

---

### Transition

Say:

> Great! You are thinking from different angles.  
> Good leaders also **look at problems from many perspectives.**

Partner, in **Team Everest Session-2**, many mentors follow the script but **miss the deeper learning objective**.  
The session is actually designed to subtly teach **three psychological ideas about leadership**.

When these are missed, the session becomes **just a game about animals** instead of a **leadership insight exercise**.

Here are the **3 things mentors often miss**.

---

# 1️⃣ The Activity Is Not About Animals — It Is About **Leadership Archetypes**

Most mentors treat the activity like a **quiz**.

Example:

Mentor:  
“Who protects animals?”

Student:  
“Lion.”

Mentor:  
“Correct.”

Then they move on.

But the real learning point is:

Different leaders are good for **different situations**.

The animals represent **leadership archetypes**.

| Animal | Leadership Type |
|------|------|
| Lion | Protector / Crisis leader |
| Owl | Advisor / Strategic thinker |
| Fox | Problem solver |
| Dog | Supportive leader |
| Ant | Hardworking execution leader |
| Elephant | Organiser |
| Monkey | Culture / morale builder |
| Bird | Visionary guide |
| Bear | Survival strategist |

The hidden idea being taught:

> Leadership is **not one personality type**.

It is **situational**.

This idea comes from **Situational Leadership Theory (Hersey & Blanchard)**.

Many mentors skip explaining this.

---

# 2️⃣ The Goal Is To Make The Student **See Themselves As A Leader**

Many mentors unconsciously position leadership as:

> "Other people become leaders."

But the session actually tries to trigger **self-identification**.

The important moment is this question:

> "If you were an animal in this society, which leader would you seek guidance from?"

A deeper follow-up question mentors often forget:

You can ask:

> Which animal do you think **you are most like**?

This question is powerful because it shifts thinking from:

Passive thinking  
→ Active self-reflection.

Students often say:

- "I'm like an ant because I work hard."
- "I'm like a dog because I help friends."

That moment builds **leadership identity**.

---

# 3️⃣ The Real Lesson: Good Leadership = **Combination of Qualities**

Many mentors accidentally leave the student with the idea:

Lion = best leader.

But the curriculum is designed to show:

No single leader type solves everything.

A good mentor should close the activity with something like:

> Sometimes we need the lion's courage,  
> sometimes the owl's wisdom,  
> sometimes the ant's hard work.

The deeper lesson:

Good leaders develop **multiple qualities**.

This connects directly to the **homework reflection exercise**.

The student then thinks:

"What qualities should I build?"

---

# 🧠 Hidden Curriculum Structure

The whole session actually follows a **psychological progression**:

| Step | Purpose |
|-----|------|
| Icebreaker | Encourage disagreement thinking |
| Animal Activity | Show leadership diversity |
| Story Activity | Connect leadership to real life |
| Homework | Encourage self-development |

It is quietly training **three leadership skills**:

1️⃣ Perspective thinking  
2️⃣ Recognising leadership behaviour  
3️⃣ Self-reflection

---

# 🌱 A Small Trick Experienced Mentors Use

At the end of the session ask:

> If our class were an animal society,  
> which student would be the lion, owl, fox, etc?

Students love this question.

It reveals **how they see each other’s strengths**.

It also builds **peer appreciation**, which improves classroom culture.

**Session-2 is secretly teaching one of the most famous leadership models used in management schools (Harvard / MIT).**

But it’s disguised as a **children’s animal game**.

Once you see it, the whole curriculum suddenly makes much more sense.

## 1) “I couldn’t disagree more” – Meaning (Tamil + simple)

**Meaning:** “நான் முழுக்க முழுக்க ஒத்துக்கவே முடியாது.” / “இதுக்கு நான் ரொம்ப strongly disagree பண்ணுறேன்.”

இந்த phrase-ல முக்கியமான **trap word**: **always**.  
**“Always”** சொன்னாலே—almost always—**we can disagree ethically**.

### Mentor one-liner
> “இந்த activity-ல statement-ல இருக்குற ‘always’ மாதிரி extreme words-ஐ break பண்ணி, balanced reason சொல்ல கற்றுக்குறோம்.”

இது leadership skill: **critical thinking + respectful disagreement**.

## 2) Icebreaker-க்கு Tight Script (உங்க style-ல, ஆனால் shorter)

நீங்க already correct-ஆ explain பண்ணிட்டீங்க. இப்போ **structure மட்டும்**.

### 30-second setup
> “நான் 1 statement சொல்றேன்.  
> நீங்க ‘Agree’ பண்ண கூடாது—**Disagree** பண்ணி **one reason** சொல்லணும்.  
> Reason ethical-ஆ இருக்கணும்.  
> Ready?”

### Rule: “One sentence only”
> “ஒரு sentence-ல சொல்லு. அதுக்கப்புறம் example.”

(இந்த rule student-oda hesitation குறைக்கும்.)

### Statement 1: “It is always good to be famous.”
**Student prompt (best):**
> “Always good இல்லன்னா—எந்த situation-ல bad ஆகும்?”

If student stuck, **give 2 options** (multiple choice):
> “A) privacy போயிடும்  
> B) pressure அதிகம்  
> Which one?”

Student picks → then ask:
> “Why?”

✅ நீங்க Ajith example போனது ok. ஆனால் **1 example மட்டும்** போதும்.

---

### Statement 2: “Homework is always helpful.”
Student prompt:
> “Homework helpful தான். But ‘always’ helpful இல்ல. When?”

If student says “too much homework stress” → perfect.

Mentor close:
> “So the reason is: **quantity matters**.”

---

### Statement 3: “Having a lot of money is the key to happiness.”
Student prompt:
> “Money helps. But happiness-க்கு only money enough இல்ல. What else is needed?”

**Avoid**: gold digger/romance examples with teens (sometimes awkward).  
Use safer examples:
- health
- peace of mind
- family/friends
- purpose

If student gives “study / knowledge” like your student did → **that is a strong answer**.

Mentor close:
> “Good. So money can buy comfort, but not meaning.”

---

### Icebreaker Mini-Wrap (this is the “companion” moment)
Ask 1 question only:
> “இப்போ நீங்க என்ன skill use பண்ணீங்க?”

Give 3 choices:
- listening
- thinking
- speaking

Let student pick 1.  
Then you add:
> “Leadership starts with **thinking clearly** and **speaking respectfully**.”

Done. **Move on.**

Then start scenarios.

### Golden rule
> “Animal + reason = answer.  
> Animal மட்டும் சொன்னா incomplete.”
