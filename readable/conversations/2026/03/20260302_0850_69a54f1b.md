You’re referring to the classic **Rule of 72** — a quick mental formula used in finance.

---

## 🔹 Rule of 72 (for 100% increase — money doubles)

\[
\textbf{Years to Double} \approx \frac{72}{\text{Interest Rate (\%)}}
\]

Example:  
If interest rate = 8%  

\[
72 ÷ 8 = 9 \text{ years}
\]

So money roughly doubles in **9 years**.

---

# 🔹 What About 200% or 300% Growth?

Now let’s go deeper.

### Important:
- 100% increase → 2× (double)
- 200% increase → 3× (triple)
- 300% increase → 4× (quadruple)

Compound growth follows logarithms, not linear scaling.

---

## 🔹 Rule of 114 (for Tripling – 200% gain)

There is a known extension:

\[
\textbf{Years to Triple} \approx \frac{114}{\text{Interest Rate}}
\]

Example: 8%

\[
114 ÷ 8 = 14.25 \text{ years}
\]

---

## 🔹 Rule of 144 (for Quadrupling – 300% gain)

\[
\textbf{Years to Quadruple} \approx \frac{144}{\text{Interest Rate}}
\]

Example: 8%

\[
144 ÷ 8 = 18 \text{ years}
\]

---

# 🔹 Why These Numbers?

Because compound growth follows:

\[
A = P(1+r)^t
\]

To solve for time:

\[
t = \frac{\ln(\text{Multiplier})}{\ln(1+r)}
\]

And:

- ln(2) ≈ 0.693 → gives Rule of 72
- ln(3) ≈ 1.098 → gives ~114
- ln(4) ≈ 1.386 → gives ~144

So these are logarithmic approximations.

---

# 🔹 Quick Mental Table

| Growth | Multiplier | Rule Number |
|--------|------------|-------------|
| 100%   | 2×         | 72          |
| 200%   | 3×         | 114         |
| 300%   | 4×         | 144         |

---

# 🔹 Big Insight

Compound growth is exponential.  
Doubling twice does NOT take double time.

At 8%:

- Double → 9 years  
- Quadruple → 18 years  

Exactly double time because 4 = 2².

But tripling is not midway — it's logarithmic.

## Universal mental formula

Let:

- **r** = annual interest rate in **%** (like 8, 12, 15)
- **G** = desired gain in **%** (like 100, 200, 500, 1000)
- **Multiplier** \( M = 1 + \frac{G}{100} \)

Then approximate years:

\[
\boxed{\text{Years} \;\approx\; \frac{69.3 \times \ln(M)}{r}}
\]

That’s it. One rule.

### Why 69.3?
Because \( \ln(2)=0.693 \) and “rule-of-72” is basically a friendly rounded cousin of \(69.3\).

---

## Make it fully mental (no calculator) with a tiny trick

Use:

\[
\ln(M)\; \approx\; 2.3 \times \log_{10}(M)
\]

So:

\[
\text{Years} \approx \frac{69.3 \times 2.3 \times \log_{10}(M)}{r}
\approx \boxed{\frac{160 \times \log_{10}(M)}{r}}
\]

### This is the **one compact mental approximation rule**:
\[
\boxed{\text{Years} \;\approx\; \frac{160 \times \log_{10}(M)}{r}}
\]

Now you only need rough base-10 logs.
