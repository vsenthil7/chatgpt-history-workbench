## 1) One structural psychological model that unifies the theories

Think of the “stop initiating → loneliness” phenomenon as a **feedback system** with 5 layers:

**A. Relationship inputs (behavioral layer)**
- Initiation frequency
- Responsiveness (reply/availability)
- Maintenance effort (planning, checking in, repair attempts)

⬇️ becomes

**B. Appraisals (cognitive layer)**
- **Equity / fairness appraisal**: “Is effort balanced?” (Equity theory)
- **Responsiveness appraisal**: “Do they ‘get’ me / care / show up?” (close-relationship literature)
- **Role appraisal**: “Am I the organizer, fixer, emotional container?”

⬇️ becomes

**C. Core social needs (motivational layer)**
- **Mattering**: “Do I register as important?”
- **Belonging / security**: “Is this bond reliable?”
- **Agency & dignity**: “Am I choosing this, or trapped doing the work?”

⬇️ becomes

**D. Affect + action tendencies (emotional/behavioral loop)**
- If equity+mattering are high → warmth, approach, continued investment
- If equity+mattering are low → resentment, sadness, withdrawal, “testing” (stop initiating)

⬇️ becomes

**E. Lifespan context (structural layer)**
- With age, **scaffolds fall away** (work/school forced contact), so maintenance becomes visible.
- **SST (Socioemotional Selectivity Theory)** shifts goals toward fewer, emotionally meaningful ties, raising the “reciprocity threshold” for what you keep.

**Key prediction of the model**
- “Stop initiating” is not a magical test; it’s a **perturbation** to the system that reveals whether the tie is held together by (1) mutual maintenance or (2) one-sided labor.

### Social connection → mortality (big, highly cited)
**Holt-Lunstad, Smith, & Layton (2010)** meta-analysis:
- Across **148 studies (N≈308,849)**: **OR = 1.50** (95% CI 1.42–1.59)  
Meaning: people with stronger social relationships had about **50% higher odds of survival** over follow-up. citeturn4view1

It also reports stronger effects for “complex social integration” measures (e.g., OR ~ 1.91) than simple markers like living alone. citeturn4view1

### Loneliness / isolation / living alone → mortality (separating constructs)

- **Social isolation OR = 1.29**
- **Loneliness OR = 1.26**
- **Living alone OR = 1.32**  
Interpreted as ~29%, 26%, 32% increased mortality likelihood respectively (in analyses with covariates). citeturn5view0turn5view1

## 3) Friendship reciprocity vs romantic reciprocity (what’s the same, what’s different)

### What’s shared (both types of bonds)
- Reciprocity works through the same psychological channels:
  - fairness/equity appraisal
  - responsiveness appraisal
  - mattering
  - trust about future support
- When reciprocity is chronically low, distress rises; people either renegotiate or withdraw.

### What differs structurally

**Friendships**
- Often **lower explicit obligation** (“we should talk” is socially weaker)
- More **context-dependent** (work, neighborhood, shared activity)
- More vulnerable to **life-friction** (moves, kids, workload)
- “Non-initiation” can mean: low care **or** high friction / low bandwidth.

**Romantic relationships**
- Higher **role entanglement** (cohabitation, finances, family coordination)
- Reciprocity is “multi-channel” (caregiving, chores, sex, emotional support)
- Attachment system is **more consistently activated**, so non-responsiveness triggers stronger threat responses.

So: the *same reciprocity engine*, but romance has **stronger coupling** (more consequences, more attachment activation).

(For romantic attachment-quality evidence + duration moderation: citeturn4view0turn2view0)

## 4) Attachment theory analysis of the “stop initiating” phenomenon

Use the two adult attachment dimensions:

### A) Anxious attachment (hyperactivation)
- Core fear: abandonment / not being valued
- Typical pattern:
  - initiates more, seeks reassurance
  - reads silence as threat
  - “stop initiating” becomes a high-stakes protest/experiment
- Emotional outcome if no one returns:
  - sharp mattering injury + rumination (“I’m not important”)

### B) Avoidant attachment (deactivation)
- Core fear: dependence / vulnerability
- Typical pattern:
  - initiates less, minimizes need
  - prefers low-maintenance ties
  - “stop initiating” feels like regaining autonomy (“fine, I won’t chase”)
- Emotional outcome if no one returns:
  - less overt distress, more narrative dismissal (“people are unreliable anyway”)
  - but loneliness can accumulate quietly over time.

### C) Secure attachment
- Expects responsiveness, but tolerates distance
- Interprets non-initiation more probabilistically (bandwidth, life stage) and will:
  - try a clean repair bid
  - or let go without identity collapse.

Meta-analytic work on adult romantic attachment shows insecurity is reliably associated with poorer relationship outcomes and that effects can grow more negative with longer relationship duration. citeturn4view0turn2view0

## 3) Life-stage model: how scores *naturally* shift (20s vs 40s vs 60s)

This is the key: **the same behavior means different things at different ages because the social environment changes.**

### Age ~20s: “High-volume, high-noise networks”
**Structural context**
- Shared institutions (college, early jobs)
- Constant ambient contact

**What tends to score higher**
- I (initiation) and R (fast replies) often high because everyone is online + proximate.

**What’s less predictive**
- SA and Rep can be lower simply because fewer serious life crises + conflict-avoidant norms.

**Rubric adjustment (interpretation)**
- Don’t over-read low SA/Rep in early 20s.
- Watch EP + ES (do they show depth and symmetry even in “fun mode”?)

### Age ~40s: “Bandwidth squeeze” decade
**Structural context**
- Career peak pressure, kids/parents caregiving, health load begins
- Friendships become **calendar-dependent**

**Typical pattern**
- Lower I/R/P is common (not necessarily low care)
- High-quality ties show up as:
  - **SA** (they show up when it matters)
  - **Rep** (they reconnect after gaps)
  - **FS/ES** (you feel remembered without performing)

**Rubric adjustment**
- Weight SA/Rep/FS more heavily (which RMI-W already does).
- Re-score over **6 months**, not 1–2.

### Age ~60s: “Network pruning + meaning focus” (SST effect)
**Structural context**
- Retirement transitions, health variability, bereavements, relocations
- Less forced contact; more intentional contact

**Typical pattern**
- Fewer friendships, but deeper ones
- R might slow (tech gaps) but EP/SA/FS often become clearer.

**Rubric adjustment**
- Don’t penalize response latency if communication style differs.
- Use “response reliability within their norm” + SA/Rep as strongest markers.
