### 0002 · CHATGPT · 2026-05-25T17:08:47Z

This has several **phishing red flags**. I would **not click** the “Login with Amazon” button in that email.

Key things that stand out:

- The sender shown is only “Amazon” — display names can be faked.
- The wording is generic and pressure-oriented (“limited access”, “verify immediately” style).
- The IP `171.188.144.200` appears unusual for a legitimate Amazon security alert format.
- Real Amazon security emails usually direct you to sign in manually through the official site/app rather than pushing a button link.
- The footer text is easy to copy and does not prove legitimacy.

## Safest way to check

Do this instead of clicking the email:

1. Open a new browser tab manually.
2. Go directly to:
   urlAmazon UKhttps://www.amazon.co.uk
3. Sign in normally.
4. Check:
   - **Your Account → Login & Security**
   - Recent sign-ins / devices
   - Any security alerts
5. If there is really an issue, Amazon will usually show it inside your account after login.

## Very important

- Do **not** enter password/OTP/card details from the email link.
- If you already clicked the link:
  - do not enter anything further
  - close the page
  - run a malware/antivirus scan if you downloaded anything

## Extra verification (recommended)

You can inspect the actual sender domain and link target:
- On desktop, hover over the “Login with Amazon” button.
- Check whether it truly goes to:
  - `amazon.co.uk`
  - `amazon.com`
  - or a legitimate Amazon-owned domain.
