### Left page — “A Father Like So”

**A Father Like So**

Father oh father, what a person you  
are meant to be.

Father oh father, when I expect so high  
you slip below what I want to  
see.

Father oh father, even though I need  
someone; you hide into the shadows  
like you were never there for me.

While you disappear I still do  
whatever it takes to latch on to your absent  
presence.

But your **wild objectives** seem to out  
my subjective idea of my  
figure of a father.

As I blame you for how I get treated,  
your **wild objections** turn it into a game.

Even though I may not be the  
perfect daughter, the laughter that  
comes from rare occasions with you  
will always stick with me.

---

### Right page

When I sleep at night I envision  
**WHY INVEST** in something that  
is hopeless, but there might be  
a chance of something real even  
if it’s fake.

My hope is a coping mechanism  
to the invisible situation that  
is **BEYOND THE CALL**, going to take  
all of us really meant  
to be.

## 22/09/2026 11:02 — What She Means by “Father”

Partner, if you mean **what Kenishka is actually saying about her father in this writing**, it is quite emotional.

She seems to be describing **a father she loves and wants a relationship with, but experiences as emotionally absent or inconsistent**.

The strongest ideas I see are:

- **“what a person you are meant to be”** — she has an idea of what she believes a father *should* be.
- **“when I expect so high / you slip below what I want to see”** — she has expectations of him, followed by disappointment.
- **“even though I need someone, you hide into the shadows”** — this sounds like wanting support or presence but feeling that he withdraws or isn't available.
- **“I still do whatever it takes to latch on to your absent presence”** — this is probably the most important line. She appears to be saying: *even when you aren't really present, I am still trying to hold on to you.*
- **“I blame you…”** — there is some anger or hurt.
- But then she ends with **“the laughter that comes from rare occasions with you will always stick with me.”** That changes the picture: there is affection and value attached to the good moments, even though she describes them as rare.

So I **wouldn't read this as “I hate my father.”** It reads much more like:

> **“I want my father. I have an idea of the father I wish you were. I feel disappointed and hurt when you're absent, but I still hold on to you and treasure the good moments we have.”**

There is also an interesting phrase in the teacher's red annotation: **“love your use of oxymoron”**, apparently referring to **“absent presence.”** That suggests the assignment may deliberately encourage figurative/creative writing. So we shouldn't automatically assume every line is a literal factual statement about home life.

If **you are the father she is writing about**, I would take the emotional message seriously, but **not treat the poem as a verdict about you**. The clearest thing she appears to be asking for through the writing is **more presence/connection**, rather than material things or perfection.
