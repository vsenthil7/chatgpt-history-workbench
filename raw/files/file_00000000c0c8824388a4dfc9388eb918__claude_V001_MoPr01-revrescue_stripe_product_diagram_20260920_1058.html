Context — what you are looking at

RevRescue recovers failed subscription payments for Stripe merchants. When a payment fails it retries at a sensible moment, reminds the customer over four days, offers a deal to anyone cancelling, records which of those actions recovered the money, and bills the merchant a share of what it recovered.

The diagram below is the whole product on one page. Colour shows how far each part has been built. The important thing to understand before reading it: nothing in RevRescue has ever talked to Stripe. That is not an oversight — it is decision D14, which parks the Stripe connectors until you take it. Everything that could be built without Stripe has been built and tested.

How to read the status words. Built means written and proven by tests that run real requests through the real application against a real database. It does not mean live: no real payment, message or alert has ever been sent.

How far the proof goes

What is complete

CapabilityStatusWhat that means in practice
Recovering a failed payment: retry timing, reminders, save offersBuiltEvery rule is tested; the scheduler drains its own queue and cannot send a reminder twice.
Knowing which payment we recovered, and proving itBuiltAttribution records the evidence; the merchant can read it, and a payment we did not act on is never billed.
Charging the merchant: monthly statement, cap, trial, creditsBuiltPer month and per currency, immutable once issued, with credits that converge exactly. Checked on 400 random histories and by mutation testing at 99.1%.
Not charging a customer twiceBuiltEvery attempt has a stable key stored before the call; after a crash the invoice state is read first.
Keeping merchants apartBuiltThe database itself filters by tenant on all 20 account tables; crafted cross-tenant reads and writes are refused.
Proving nothing was alteredBuiltEach audit row is chained to the previous one; the merchant can verify their own trail, and edits are detected.
Not losing workBuiltRepeated failures park the work with a dead letter that an authenticated operator can replay or discard.
Privacy promises: export, delete, opt-out, retentionBuiltDelete covers every table holding account data, opt-outs survive restarts and stop both channels, retention runs on a schedule.
Access control: roles, revocation, team changesBuiltRemoving or demoting someone ends their session at once; the last owner cannot be removed.
Safety net around the build itselfBuiltSeven checks fail the build, including money rules and a check that published promises have code behind them.

What is not complete, and why

CapabilityStatusWhy not
Sending a single real payment retry, reminder or alertNot builtThe Stripe, email, WhatsApp and Slack connectors are all parked by decision D14. Building fakes instead would make every test meaningless.
Proving Stripe honours our idempotency keyNot builtOnly Stripe can prove it. Needs D14 and a test account.
The merchant screens inside the Stripe DashboardNot builtThe UI runs inside Stripe and needs an app registration. Needs D14.
The app listing and its manifestNot builtNeeds the app registration, the final price, and a penetration test.
The fee percentageNot builtIt is a placeholder everywhere on purpose. The pricing model is your decision, and the independent review called it the weakest part of the plan.
Proving we only bill recoveries we causedPartly builtToday a payment is billed if we acted first. Proving the customer would not have paid anyway needs a holdback experiment, which needs your authorisation.
Billing the merchant for our own feeNot builtHow merchants pay us is undecided (DEC-02), so nothing triggers grace mode or read-only either.
Operator screensNot builtThe operator API works and is audited; a screen has simply not been built yet. Nothing blocks it.
Enterprise sign-in (SSO/SCIM)Not builtNo current merchant asks for it and it is large; ranked below launch.
Production infrastructure: hosting, log retention, shared rate limits, SRE targetsNot builtNo hosting has been chosen. The current rate limit is per server instance and says so.
An outside opinion beyond one AI reviewPartly builtOne independent AI review has been done and answered. A developer-led review and a penetration test are still outstanding.

The short list that unblocks everything

  1. DEC-04 — unpark the Stripe connectors and provide a test account. This alone releases the red boxes.
  2. Pricing model — percentage of recovered revenue, a flat tier, or incremental pricing. The fee is a placeholder until you decide, and the independent review called this the weakest part of the plan.
  3. Confirm the model with Stripe in writing before the listing is prepared.
  4. Authorise the holdback experiment if you want to claim that RevRescue caused a recovery rather than merely acted first.
  5. Three short approvals: the corrected help and FAQ wording, the retention periods, and who may act as an operator.
