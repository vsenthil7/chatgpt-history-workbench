4. Measure before assigning scores
The useful distinction in the pasted discussion is knowledge, ownership, and AI leverage. I would retain that framework and make it observable.

Dimension	Assessment	Evidence required
Knowledge	Explain a workflow and predict what happens when a component fails before asking AI	Accurate reasoning, including uncertainty and boundaries
Ownership	Diagnose a seeded defect, choose a correction, and validate it	Logs, code paths, test results, and explanation of why the fix works
Leverage	Use AI to complete a bounded task, then inspect and defend the result	Working outcome, time taken, defects found, and effective review
Architectural judgement	Compare two designs under explicit cost, security, and reliability constraints	A justified decision—not a list of fashionable tools
Operational competence	Recover from failure and explain what users experienced	Recovery evidence, monitoring, and a prevention plan
For TalkProof, suitable exercises include duplicate webhooks, incorrect tenant resolution, retrieval errors, tool loops, failed providers, and unexpected cost increases. These are proposed exercises—not claims that those defects currently exist.

Teaching before an exercise measures learning. A baseline must first capture what you can do without that teaching. We should keep the two separate.
