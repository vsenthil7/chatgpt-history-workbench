This document has been created because the project did not begin as a clean software-product idea.

It began as a practical discussion about:

- starting or strengthening a BPO,
- women working from home,
- flexible part-time availability,
- diallers,
- Indian telecom providers,
- customer support,
- outbound calling,
- client pricing,
- worker payments,
- call recording,
- Tamil transcription,
- CRM and helpdesk software,
- quality review,
- knowledge transfer,
- emergency calls,
- capacity planning,
- client acquisition,
- freelancer participation,
- partner companies,
- and eventually the future of work in an AI-driven economy.

The discussion moved backwards and forwards because each practical problem exposed a deeper structural problem.

The intention of this document is not to pretend that every idea is proven. Its purpose is to preserve:

1. where each idea came from,
2. why it emerged,
3. what has been discovered,
4. which parts are strong principles,
5. which parts remain hypotheses,
6. which futuristic ideas should not yet become requirements,
7. what other contributors are being asked to examine,
8. and how contributions should be added without destroying the discovery history.

# 5. Discovery methodology used so far

The model emerged through iterative founder reasoning rather than a conventional workshop.

The repeated pattern was:

1. identify a practical problem,
2. challenge the conventional assumption,
3. introduce an alternative operating model,
4. identify a new hidden dependency,
5. expand the concept,
6. then return and test it using another example.

This method has been messy, but productive.

It should now be preserved through a formal discovery system so future contributors do not:

- repeat the same questions,
- treat hypotheses as decisions,
- omit the reasoning,
- or create a new unrelated product from a short summary.

## DISC-001 — Flexible Workforce Availability Model

### Origin

The initial social-enterprise idea assumed women would work from home and may not be continuously available.

The original statement was effectively:

> A woman may work when she has time, and her available time may vary from week to week or day to day.

### Conventional assumption challenged

A normal BPO assumes:

- fixed shifts,
- named agents,
- predictable attendance,
- one employee working continuously,
- and client capacity expressed in employees or seats.

That model does not naturally fit fragmented household availability.

### Discovery

Worker time must be captured in multiple forms:

1. **Preferred availability**  
   The worker is generally available, but it is not guaranteed.

2. **Weekly confirmed availability**  
   The worker confirms specific blocks for the next week.

3. **Daily reconfirmation**  
   The worker confirms whether tomorrow’s planned availability remains valid.

4. **Committed service block**  
   The worker reserves a block that the company may sell to clients.

5. **Emergency reserve**  
   The worker agrees to be called under specific urgent conditions.

6. **Flexible asynchronous capacity**  
   The worker can complete work before a deadline without being available at a specific minute.

### Why it matters

The company cannot calculate:

- sellable capacity,
- SLA coverage,
- client commitments,
- worker pay,
- or backup requirements

without a reliable availability model.

### Product implication

The platform requires:

- availability capture,
- weekly planning,
- daily confirmation,
- change management,
- attendance/adherence,
- backup invitation,
- availability confidence,
- and actual-versus-planned analysis.

### Status

**Strong Discovery**

### Questions remaining

- How far in advance must committed blocks be booked?
- What cancellation rules are fair?
- Should committed blocks receive minimum guaranteed payment?
- How should household emergencies be accommodated?
- How is reliability calculated without punishing legitimate caregiving responsibilities?

## DISC-002 — Capacity Instead of Headcount

### Origin

The conversation initially asked:

> How many agents are required?

This was found to be the wrong primary question.

### Discovery

The more useful question is:

> How much reliable, skill-qualified, time-qualified service capacity is available?

Capacity may be measured as:

- calling minutes,
- connected-call handling minutes,
- callback tasks,
- support hours,
- WhatsApp conversations,
- email tickets,
- validated records,
- candidate screenings,
- test cases,
- software work objects,
- review hours,
- language-specific support windows,
- or client-certified work capacity.

### Example

A network may contain 50 registered women, but this does not mean 50 agents are sellable.

After deducting:

- unavailable time,
- unreliable availability,
- training,
- skill mismatch,
- language mismatch,
- quality restrictions,
- backup reserve,
- and already-sold capacity,

the sellable capacity may be much smaller.

### Product implication

The platform requires a capacity engine that calculates:

```text
Gross Availability
- Reliability Adjustment
- Non-Delivery Time
- Skill Constraints
- Client Certification Constraints
- Reserve Capacity
- Existing Commitments
= Sellable Capacity
```

### Strategic implication

Client acquisition must be constrained by actual sellable capacity.

### Status

**Very Strong Discovery**

### Future possibility

Capacity may become visible as an organisational asset similar to:

- cash,
- inventory,
- machine hours,
- cloud capacity,
- or available seats.

## DISC-003 — Supply-Aware Client Acquisition

### Origin

The founder explicitly rejected the sequence:

> Acquire any client first, then somehow create workforce.

### Discovery

During the early stage, the operation should be partially supply-led:

```text
Register Workers
→ Assess Skills
→ Collect Availability
→ Train and Certify
→ Calculate Reliable Capacity
→ Identify Suitable Client Demand
→ Sell Only Matching Service Capacity
```

Traditional demand-led recruitment may still be used later for larger contracts.

### Product implication

The sales function requires a **capacity gate**.

A prospect should be classified:

- **Green:** Can accept now.
- **Amber:** Can accept after training, recruitment or additional software.
- **Red:** Cannot responsibly accept.

### Capacity approval checks

- time window,
- service volume,
- language,
- skill,
- client certification,
- SLA,
- emergency reserve,
- software concurrency,
- data-security requirement,
- profitability,
- and concentration risk.

### Status

**Strong Discovery**

## DISC-004 — Callback-First Inbound Service

### Origin

The early discussion assumed inbound support requires workers waiting for calls.

The founder challenged this by referring to bank callback queues.

### Discovery

Inbound service can operate through multiple models:

1. **Live answer**
2. **Queue callback**
3. **Scheduled callback**
4. **Hybrid urgent/live and normal/callback**
5. **Voice-message-to-task**
6. **IVR or conversational intake followed by callback**

For many SME use cases, immediate live answer may not be necessary.

### Suitable callback-first services

- sales enquiries,
- appointment booking,
- recruitment enquiries,
- education admissions,
- real-estate follow-up,
- product information,
- delivery complaints,
- general customer feedback,
- and non-critical support.

### Product implication

An inbound call can create a Work Object containing:

- caller identity,
- number,
- client,
- intent,
- recording,
- transcript,
- urgency,
- callback deadline,
- required skill,
- and customer history.

### Commercial implication

Clients can buy response SLAs:

- callback within 4 hours,
- 1 hour,
- 30 minutes,
- 15 minutes,
- or live answer.

### Status

**Strong Discovery**

### Boundary

This model is unsuitable as the sole method for:

- life-threatening emergencies,
- active fraud requiring immediate blocking,
- severe security incidents,
- or other cases requiring real-time authority.

## DISC-005 — Live Coverage and Reserved Capacity

### Origin

The founder argued that even urgent and live services may be supported by pooling workers and keeping a buffer.

### Discovery

Capacity must be divided into:

- sold productive capacity,
- sold reserved coverage,
- flexible capacity,
- emergency reserve,
- backup capacity,
- and unsold capacity.

The system should not sell 100% of theoretical availability.

### Example

If 100 hours are available, the company may sell only 70–80 hours and retain the rest for:

- demand spikes,
- worker absence,
- urgent calls,
- training,
- or service recovery.

### Product implication

Workers need real-time states:

- available,
- busy,
- reserved,
- break,
- training,
- offline,
- and unavailable.

### Commercial implication

Reserved availability has economic value even when no call occurs.

The worker must not be paid only when a call happens.

### Status

**Strong Discovery**

## DISC-006 — Unified Multi-Client Workforce Pool

### Origin

The traditional model assigns one agent to one client.

The proposed social-enterprise model requires workers to serve multiple clients.

### Discovery

Workers belong primarily to a shared capacity pool, subject to:

- skills,
- languages,
- client certifications,
- security permissions,
- priority,
- and current workload.

### Benefit

This can improve utilisation and allow small clients to buy partial capacity.

### Major risk

A worker must not serve a client without:

- sufficient knowledge,
- permission,
- current process version,
- and complete customer history.

### Product implication

The system requires:

- tenant separation,
- client certification,
- contextual screen pop,
- knowledge delivery,
- assignment controls,
- and conflict-of-interest rules.

### Status

**Strong Discovery**

### Origin

In a dedicated-agent model, knowledge builds invisibly in the worker’s memory.

In a shared-workforce model, tomorrow’s worker may be different.

### Discovery

Knowledge must belong to the system, not the individual.

### Required knowledge

- client profile,
- products,
- services,
- scripts,
- policies,
- FAQs,
- escalation rules,
- customer timeline,
- prior interactions,
- open issues,
- commitments,
- prohibited statements,
- versions,
- and approved examples.

### Product implication

Every worker must receive contextual information at the moment of work.

### Organisational implication

Documentation is not an administrative side task. It is a delivery requirement.

### Status

**Strong Discovery**

### Future thought

Company knowledge may become a portable, versioned and commercially measurable asset.

## DISC-008 — Universal Recursive Work Object

### Origin

The discussion asked what the unit of work should be.

“Task” was too narrow, and “work unit” suggested something indivisible.

### Breakthrough

The proposed universal concept is:

> **Work Object**

A Work Object can contain child Work Objects recursively.

### Examples

#### BPO

```text
Customer Enquiry
  ├── Call Interaction
  ├── Transcript
  ├── Disposition
  ├── Follow-Up
  ├── Transcript Validation
  └── QA Review
```

#### Software

```text
Epic
  └── Story
      ├── Design
      ├── Backend
      ├── Frontend
      ├── Unit Test
      ├── Code Review
      ├── Playwright Test
      └── Deployment Verification
```

#### Content

```text
YouTube Video
  ├── Research
  ├── Script
  ├── Review
  ├── Recording
  ├── Editing
  ├── Thumbnail
  ├── Upload
  └── Analytics Review
```

### Core attributes

A Work Object may contain:

- identity,
- parent,
- type,
- objective,
- inputs,
- outputs,
- owner,
- status,
- priority,
- SLA,
- dependencies,
- skills,
- language,
- estimated effort,
- actual effort,
- cost,
- value,
- evidence,
- review requirements,
- approval rules,
- and audit history.

### Status

**Breakthrough Discovery**

### Open question

Can one universal object remain useful across industries without becoming excessively generic?

## DISC-009 — Review, Approval and Escalation Are Work

### Origin

Transcript validation, QA and management review were initially treated as secondary steps.

### Discovery

Each review is independently assignable work.

Examples:

- transcript validation,
- call QA,
- manager approval,
- compliance review,
- code review,
- UAT,
- content review,
- financial approval,
- and escalation investigation.

### Automatic generation

A completed Work Object may create child review objects based on:

- low confidence,
- risk,
- random sample,
- new worker status,
- policy violation,
- complaint,
- critical field,
- or client rule.

### Example

```text
Call Completed
→ AI Detects Possible Policy Violation
→ QA Review Work Object
→ Team Leader Reviews
→ If Confirmed and Severe
→ Manager Approval Work Object
→ Coaching / Corrective Action / Client Escalation
```

### Status

**Breakthrough Discovery**

### AI-era implication

AI may reduce creation effort while increasing:

- verification,
- exception handling,
- approval,
- and judgement work.

## DISC-010 — Priority, SLA and Pre-emption

### Origin

The founder proposed that an urgent inbound case should interrupt a low-priority outbound task.

### Discovery

Not all work is equal.

The platform requires:

- priority classes,
- SLA deadlines,
- pre-emption rules,
- emergency reserve,
- escalation paths,
- and human override.

### Example priority classes

| Priority | Example | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| P0 Critical | Immediate danger, active fraud, severe security incident | Immediate specialist escalation |
| P1 Urgent | Account blocked, major outage, severe time-sensitive failure | Immediate or minutes |
| P2 High | Time-sensitive complaint or operational failure | Short response window |
| P3 Standard | Normal support or appointment | Hours |
| P4 Planned | Sales and follow-up | Same day or scheduled |
| P5 Batch | Data work or research | Deadline based |

### Pre-emption

Not every task may be interrupted.

A work-type matrix is required.

### Status

**Strong Discovery**

### Open issue

The operating ethics of deliberately closing a low-priority customer conversation early must be carefully designed. The customer experience must not be damaged by the priority system.

## DISC-011 — Human-in-the-Loop AI

### Origin

The founder proposed using:

- a structured decision table first,
- AI when the table cannot classify,
- then a human,
- and finally updating controlled knowledge.

### Discovery

The correct decision model is:

```text
Rules
→ AI Recommendation
→ Human Review Where Required
→ Approved Decision
→ Controlled Knowledge Update
```

### AI should support

- transcription,
- summarisation,
- intent detection,
- entity extraction,
- priority suggestion,
- knowledge retrieval,
- quality flags,
- and response drafting.

### AI should not independently own

- irreversible medical decisions,
- financial fraud decisions,
- safety-critical actions,
- worker discipline,
- legal conclusions,
- or other high-risk decisions.

### Status

**Strong Discovery / Emerging Principle**

## DISC-012 — Transcript, Speech and Quality Workflow

### Origin

Tamil and Tamil-English transcription limitations were discussed in relation to ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and speech APIs.

### Discovery

Speech transcription is not one task.

It contains:

- audio capture,
- streaming or batch STT,
- partial transcript,
- final transcript,
- speaker separation,
- field verification,
- transcript validation,
- summary,
- intent classification,
- quality review,
- and storage.

### Risk-based transcript review

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| High-confidence ordinary call | No full human review |
| Low STT confidence | Create validation Work Object |
| Names, numbers, dates, amounts | Agent verifies structured fields |
| Complaint or regulated process | Human reviewer |
| Random quality sample | Team leader review |
| AI-detected violation | Mandatory QA |
| Severe issue | Manager/compliance escalation |

### Strategic implication

It is impossible to manually review 100% of thousands of calls economically.

The operating model should use:

- 100% automated scanning,
- risk-based selection,
- random sampling,
- heavier review for new workers,
- complaint-triggered review,
- and critical-field validation.

### Status

**Strong Discovery**

## DISC-013 — Worker TEE-to-TEM Economics

### Origin

The founder described:

> Time, Energy and Effort leading to Money.

### Discovery

Worker payment should not be based only on completed calls.

A blended formula may include:

```text
Committed-Time Payment
+ Productive-Work Payment
+ Skill Premium
+ Language Premium
+ Quality Bonus
+ Reliability Bonus
+ Outcome Incentive
+ Training Payment
```

### Why

This recognises:

- reserved availability,
- learning,
- quality,
- reliability,
- difficult skills,
- and productive output.

### Social-enterprise concern

The payment model must not:

- pressure workers to create unnecessary activity,
- penalise caregiving realities unfairly,
- encourage rushed low-quality calls,
- or shift all commercial risk onto workers.

### Status

**Strong Discovery / Commercial Hypothesis**

### Requires

- legal classification review,
- minimum wage review,
- payroll review,
- and pilot economics.

## DISC-017 — AI Fragmentation of Jobs into Work

### Origin

The founder asked whether the product could reduce fear created by AI.

### Hypothesis

AI may not simply remove work. It may fragment jobs.

Example:

```text
Traditional Accountant Role
→ Data Entry Automated
→ Reconciliation Assisted
→ Human Validation
→ Exception Handling
→ Review
→ Approval
→ Client Communication
```

### Consequence

Current employment systems are designed around:

- one role,
- one company,
- one person,
- one fixed schedule.

AI may create a need to organise:

- smaller work objects,
- mixed human-and-AI execution,
- specialist review,
- and short-duration judgement.

### Product opportunity

DWOS could become an infrastructure for allocating fragmented work to suitable human capacity.

### Status

**Important Hypothesis — Not Validated**

# 19. Current strongest product hypothesis

The current strongest hypothesis is:

> A platform can convert work demand into recursive, governed Work Objects and allocate those Work Objects to available, qualified and commercially viable human or AI capacity while preserving knowledge, quality, evidence, worker earnings, client pricing and auditability.

This is not yet a validated product claim.

# 20. Current strongest social-enterprise hypothesis

> Fragmented time and underused capability can be combined into reliable enterprise service capacity when availability, knowledge, quality, pricing and handover are systematised.

This must be tested with real women, real availability and real service delivery.

---

# 21. Current strongest AI-era hypothesis

> AI may increasingly fragment jobs into creation, validation, review, exception handling and approval work. A platform that orchestrates these smaller work objects may reduce some of the instability and fear created by AI-driven job redesign.

This is a future-facing hypothesis and should not be marketed as proven.
