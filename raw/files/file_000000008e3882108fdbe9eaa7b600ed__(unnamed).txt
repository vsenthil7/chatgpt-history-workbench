## 1. Executive Summary

TalkProof is a certified communications and verification SaaS platform operated by WET (Women Empowerment Team), a social enterprise whose mission is the economic empowerment of women through skilled, dignified remote work. TalkProof addresses a structurally under-served gap in the compliance and contact-centre market: the ability to prove, beyond reasonable doubt, exactly what a human agent said to a client during a regulated call — and to deliver that proof, as a tamper-proof certified document, to the client themselves.

The platform combines three product pillars:

1. **Certified Voice Campaigns** — campaign management, agent call workflow, multi-model AI consensus verification, and blockchain-anchored certified PDF output, gated by Human-in-the-Loop reviewer approval.
2. **Real-Time Communications (RTC) Suite** — WhatsApp-style PSTN calling + chat/messaging, and Zoom/Teams-style audio/video meetings with recording, transcription, and AI summaries.
3. **Enterprise SaaS Platform** — multi-tenant architecture with three isolation tiers (shared pool / dedicated schema / single-tenant), role-based access control, per-IP forensic audit trail, BYO API keys, KMS encryption, Stripe billing, and white-label reseller capabilities.

WET sells and operates TalkProof as the platform super-admin, onboarding client organisations as tenants. This pairs a defensible, market-leading technology product with a social impact workforce model: WET's women agents are the human callers, and TalkProof's AI verifies those humans — the opposite of AI voice agent vendors who seek to replace human labour.

A 97-competitor analysis has confirmed five structural market gaps that TalkProof uniquely closes. No single existing product offers multi-model consensus verification, outward-facing certified PDF proof-of-delivery, TCPA call-verification (as opposed to document-consent), and a multi-tenant BPO-resellable SaaS in one integrated platform.

## 3. Problem Statement

### 3.1 The Core Problem

Regulated industries — insurance, healthcare, financial services, utilities, government — conduct millions of outbound calls daily in which agents must deliver exact verbal disclosures. When disputes arise (customer claims "I was never told X", agent's employer claims "the agent delivered the exact disclosure"), there is currently no independent, tamper-proof record of what was actually said that flows from the operator to the client as a deliverable. The result:

- **Regulatory and legal exposure**: TCPA violations alone generate $300M+/year in US legal liability.
- **Trust deficit**: Clients have no independent verification; they must take the operator's word for it.
- **Operational inefficiency**: Manual QA reviews cover at most 5–10% of calls; AI-powered tools verify 100% but rely on a single model that can be challenged.

### 3.2 Secondary Problem: Fragmented Communications

BPO operators and compliance teams currently run disparate tools — a contact-centre platform (for calls), a messaging app (for team collaboration), a video meeting tool, and a compliance recorder — each generating its own silo of data, logs, and billing. There is no single unified platform that combines certified call compliance with a full communications suite.

### 3.3 The WET Platform Problem

WET, as an impact-sourcing social enterprise operating call agents, requires a platform it can operate, brand, and resell — not merely use as a single tenant. Existing compliance recording platforms (Global Relay, Verint, Red Box) are:
- Enterprise-only, priced $50K–$1M/year;
- Single-tenant, finance-focused;
- Not designed for multi-tenant BPO resale;
- Not accessible to boutique or mid-market BPOs.

### 3.4 Problem Summary Table

| # | Problem | Impact | Affected Party |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-1 | No outward-facing tamper-proof proof-of-delivery for regulated calls | Legal/regulatory liability | BPOs, regulated operators, end clients |
| P-2 | Single-model AI verification is legally challengeable | Evidential weakness | Compliance teams, regulated clients |
| P-3 | TCPA consent documentation is document-focused, not call-verification focused | $300M+/yr liability exposure | Outbound call operators (US) |
| P-4 | Fragmented tooling (CCaaS + chat + video + recorder) | Operational overhead, data silos | BPO operators |
| P-5 | Compliance SaaS inaccessible to mid-market / BPO resellers | Market gap for WET | WET, boutique BPOs |

## 6. Business Requirements (BR-01 – BR-10)

### BR-01 — Multi-Tenant SaaS Operation by WET

**Statement**: WET shall operate TalkProof as a multi-tenant SaaS platform and shall be able to onboard, manage, suspend, and offboard client tenants independently.

**Rationale**: WET's business model depends on selling and operating the platform for multiple client organisations simultaneously. Without multi-tenancy and a robust super-admin console, WET cannot serve more than one client or scale revenue.

**Priority (MoSCoW)**: Must Have

**Modules**: M01, M09

**Acceptance Criteria**:
- AC-01.1: WET super-admin can create a new tenant with plan, quota, and rate-card configuration within 5 minutes.
- AC-01.2: WET super-admin can suspend a tenant, causing all tenant user logins to fail immediately.
- AC-01.3: WET super-admin can export all tenant data (GDPR-compliant) prior to offboarding.
- AC-01.4: Platform metrics dashboard (MRR, active tenants, seats, API spend, SLA) are visible to WET super-admin in real time.
- AC-01.5: Audited impersonation allows WET super-admin to login-as a tenant user with mandatory reason and time-box.

### BR-02 — Certified Outbound Voice Campaigns

**Statement**: Clients shall be able to run outbound voice campaigns in which each call is recorded, transcribed, verified by a multi-model AI consensus engine, and produces a tamper-proof, blockchain-anchored certified PDF report that is gated by Human-in-the-Loop reviewer approval before issuance.

**Rationale**: This is TalkProof's primary differentiating product. The certified PDF flows outward to the client (the call recipient's organisation) as proof that a disclosure was delivered — the first such product in the market. The multi-model consensus (three independent AI models) creates a "three independent witnesses" standard that is court-defensible.

**Priority (MoSCoW)**: Must Have

**Modules**: M11, M06, M07

**Acceptance Criteria**:
- AC-02.1: A campaign manager can create a campaign, assign contacts and agents, and launch within the web UI.
- AC-02.2: Agent receives call assignment, dials using the in-app dialer (PSTN), and records the call automatically.
- AC-02.3: After call completion, transcription and three-model consensus verification complete within 3 minutes.
- AC-02.4: The consensus result (scored verdict, confidence, evidence citations) is presented to a reviewer for approval.
- AC-02.5: A reviewer approves the certificate; the system issues a brand-typeset certified PDF with embedded hash, RFC3161 timestamp, and blockchain anchor reference.
- AC-02.6: The certified PDF is verifiable offline (hash + timestamp verify without platform access).
- AC-02.7: TCPA disclosure proof-of-delivery is captured: the exact disclosure text delivered verbatim is recorded immutably.

**Statement**: Users on the platform shall be able to communicate in real time via PSTN calls (WhatsApp-style, to any phone number), chat and messaging (1:1 and group, with voice notes and media), and audio/video meetings (Zoom/Teams-style, with screen share, recording, and AI summaries).

**Rationale**: BPO operators currently use 3–4 separate tools for communications. Consolidating into one platform with unified presence, a single audit trail, and metered billing reduces operational overhead and increases platform stickiness.

**Priority (MoSCoW)**: Must Have

**Acceptance Criteria**:

- AC-03.2: Chat messages deliver in real time (< 500ms P95 latency) with presence, typing indicators, and read receipts.
- AC-03.3: An audio/video meeting with up to 50 participants can be started from within the app (LiveKit SFU).
- AC-03.4: Meeting recordings (composite and per-track) are stored in S3 and accessible post-meeting.

### BR-04 — Human-in-the-Loop Gating for Consequential Actions

**Statement**: All consequential actions — most critically, the issuance of certified call certificates — shall be gated by a Human-in-the-Loop (HiL) approval workflow. AI outputs shall be advisory with confidence and evidence; they shall never auto-execute consequential actions.

**Rationale**: Regulated industries, and the legal defensibility of certified outputs, require that a human reviewer explicitly approves before a certificate is issued. This also provides Separation of Duties (SoD): the proposer cannot be their own approver. The HiL design aligns with WET's mission of augmenting human workers rather than replacing them.

**Priority (MoSCoW)**: Must Have

**Modules**: M06, M07

**Acceptance Criteria**:
- AC-04.1: Certificate issuance is routed to a reviewer queue; a certificate cannot be issued without explicit approval by a user in the Reviewer role or above.
- AC-04.2: Self-approval is refused by the system; the proposer cannot approve their own proposal.
- AC-04.3: Dual-control (maker-checker) is enforced for any action configured with a threshold > 1 approver.
- AC-04.4: Reviewer queue shows SLA timers; escalation fires if SLA expires without action.
- AC-04.5: Every approval decision records who approved, when, why (mandatory reason field), and the approver's IP address.
- AC-04.6: A rollback token is issued on approval-executed actions, enabling reversal within a defined window.

### BR-05 — Forensic Audit Trail with Per-IP Source Logging

**Statement**: Every access event, data mutation, and authentication event on the platform shall be logged to a forensic, append-only, hash-chained audit trail that captures the source IP address, user identity, tenant, role, route, method, and timestamp. The trail shall be tamper-detectable via a chain-verification function.

**Rationale**: Regulated industries require audit evidence for SOC2, GDPR, HIPAA, and legal discovery. The per-IP trail is specifically required to prove that a particular user accessed data from a particular location — critical when certificates or consent records are challenged. WORM retention and SIEM export are required for enterprise clients.

**Priority (MoSCoW)**: Must Have

**Modules**: M04

**Acceptance Criteria**:
- AC-05.1: Every HTTP request writes an `access_trail` row containing: user_id, tenant_id, role, route, method, source IP (X-Forwarded-For resolved), geo, user-agent, session_id, referrer, latency, and response status.
- AC-05.2: Every mutation writes an `audit_log` row with prev_hash + row_hash (SHA-256), making tampering detectable.
- AC-05.3: `verify_chain()` function confirms chain integrity; any broken link is surfaced as a tamper alert.
- AC-05.4: Auth events (login, MFA challenge, SSO, impersonation, logout) are logged with IP and device fingerprint.
- AC-05.5: Admin dashboard allows filtering by user, tenant, IP, route, and date range.
- AC-05.6: Signed-webhook SIEM export to Splunk / Microsoft Sentinel is available to enterprise tenants.
- AC-05.7: WORM (Write-Once Read-Many) retention option ensures audit records cannot be deleted.

### BR-06 — Client-Selectable Data-Isolation Tier

**Statement**: Clients shall be able to choose their data-isolation tier — shared pool (Tier A), dedicated schema (Tier B), or single-tenant deployment (Tier C) — based on their compliance and cost requirements, with WET provisioning the appropriate tier.

**Rationale**: A one-size-fits-all isolation model either over-charges starter clients (if everything is single-tenant) or under-protects enterprise/regulated clients (if everything is shared). The tiered model allows WET to serve the full market — from freemium BPOs to HIPAA-regulated healthcare operators — at appropriate price points.

**Priority (MoSCoW)**: Must Have

**Modules**: M01

**Acceptance Criteria**:
- AC-06.1: A Tier A tenant cannot read, write, or enumerate any data belonging to a different tenant (RLS enforced at the database layer).
- AC-06.2: A Tier B tenant's data resides in a dedicated database schema; a cross-schema query from another tenant's ORM session is blocked.
- AC-06.3: A Tier C tenant receives a standalone deployment (own DB, own app instance); WET can deploy from a parameterised IaC manifest in < 2 hours.
- AC-06.4: WET can migrate a tenant from Tier A → B or B → C without data loss or service interruption > 30 minutes.

### BR-07 — Bring-Your-Own Provider API Keys

**Statement**: Tenants shall be able to supply their own API keys for LLM providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini), telephony carriers (Twilio, Telnyx, Vonage), speech-to-text services, and video infrastructure (LiveKit), with the platform routing to their keys and metering usage against their accounts.

**Rationale**: Enterprise clients require that their data is processed under their own provider contracts and DPAs, not the platform operator's. BYO keys also allow large clients to negotiate their own volume pricing and to satisfy data residency requirements. Platform-key fallback (metered) ensures that tenants without BYO keys can still use the platform.

**Priority (MoSCoW)**: Should Have

**Modules**: M05

**Acceptance Criteria**:
- AC-07.1: Tenant admin can add an API key for each supported provider; the key is encrypted at rest (KMS/Fernet) and is never returned in plaintext after storage.
- AC-07.2: A "test connection" action verifies the key is valid before saving.
- AC-07.3: All API calls for that tenant route through the tenant's key; metered usage and cost are tracked per key.
- AC-07.4: Spend caps can be configured per key; an alert fires when 80% of the cap is reached and requests are blocked at 100%.
- AC-07.5: Platform-key fallback is used and metered when the tenant has no BYO key configured for a given provider.

### BR-08 — Enterprise-Grade Security, Compliance, and Billing

**Statement**: The platform shall implement enterprise-grade authentication (SSO/MFA/passkeys), authorisation (RBAC), encryption (KMS envelope encryption + per-tenant keys), compliance readiness (SOC2/GDPR/TCPA/HIPAA), and a fully automated billing system (Stripe — freemium/premium/enterprise tiers with usage metering, invoicing, and dunning).

**Rationale**: Without this requirement, TalkProof cannot serve regulated enterprise clients, cannot achieve SOC2 Type II, and cannot be sold to clients who require GDPR data processing agreements or HIPAA BAAs. Billing automation is required for WET to operate at scale without manual invoice generation.

**Priority (MoSCoW)**: Must Have

**Modules**: M02, M03, M08, M10, M19, M20

**Acceptance Criteria**:
- AC-08.1: Users can authenticate via email+password (Argon2), TOTP MFA, WebAuthn passkey, OIDC SSO (Entra/Okta/Google), or SAML SSO.
- AC-08.2: SCIM provisioning automatically creates/disables users when an enterprise directory changes.
- AC-08.3: Role hierarchy (SuperAdmin > TenantOwner > TenantAdmin > Manager > Reviewer > Agent > Member > Auditor) is enforced server-side on every API call.
- AC-08.4: All data at rest is KMS envelope-encrypted with per-tenant keys; key rotation is supported.
- AC-08.5: Stripe integration handles plan checkout, subscription lifecycle, usage-based metering, invoice generation, and dunning without manual intervention.
- AC-08.6: GDPR data-subject export and erasure complete within 72 hours of request.
- AC-08.7: TCPA outbound consent is enforced — a call to a number without recorded consent is blocked.
- AC-08.8: SOC2 control mapping evidence is maintained in a continuously updated evidence store.

## 11. Risks

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | Multi-model consensus is challenged in court as insufficiently independent (models share training data). | Medium | High | Document model independence; use models from ≥ 2 distinct vendors; commission legal opinion. |
| R-02 | A key third-party provider (e.g., Twilio, OpenAI) raises prices, changes terms, or goes offline. | Medium | High | BYO keys model reduces dependency; provider abstraction layer (M05) supports multi-provider routing and fallback. |
| R-03 | TCPA regulatory interpretation changes, reducing the value of call-level proof. | Low | High | Monitor TCPA guidance; platform captures maximum evidence regardless; consult legal counsel in each launch market. |
| R-04 | Data breach or tenant cross-contamination in Tier A (shared pool). | Low | Critical | PostgreSQL RLS + forced tenant-scoping dependency (M01); regular pen testing; security scanning (M20). |
| R-05 | WET agent workforce cannot scale fast enough to meet certified campaign demand. | Medium | Medium | Graduated hiring plan; generic-client tenant enables flexible staffing. |
| R-06 | Enterprise clients reject shared-pool (Tier A) for GDPR compliance, slowing Professional tier adoption. | Medium | Medium | Tier B (dedicated schema) is available at Professional+; migrate existing clients on request. |
| R-07 | Mobile app rejected by Apple App Store due to PSTN calling or recording features. | Low | High | Review App Store guidelines pre-submission; provide web fallback for affected features; legal review of recording disclosure requirements. |
| R-08 | Competitor replicates multi-model consensus feature within 12 months. | Medium | Medium | Maintain 18-month technology lead; file patents where applicable; embed into regulated-client long-term contracts. |
