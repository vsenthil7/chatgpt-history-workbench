# AI Career Target Decision & Git Architecture

**Decision date:** 08/10/2026 15:06\
**Status:** DECIDED for preparation through 15 November 2026\
**Decision owner:** Senthil\
**Inputs:** independent proposals from ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity;
current UK role evidence reviewed by ChatGPT.

## 1. Decision

### Primary target

**Senior AI Engineer --- Agentic & Generative AI Systems**

Use these as equivalent/search titles rather than separate career
tracks: - Senior AI Engineer --- Agentic AI - AI Engineer --- LLM &
Agentic Systems - Applied AI Engineer --- Agentic/LLM Systems - AI/ML
Engineer --- Agentic AI & LLMs - GenAI / Agentic AI Engineer

### Secondary/stretch target

**Applied ML / ML Engineer --- LLM, Search & Agents**

This is a stretch lane, not the preparation anchor. It becomes a primary
lane only if the November evidence shows sufficient depth in statistics,
classical ML, model evaluation/training and MLOps.

### Explicitly excluded as destination

-   Data Engineer
-   Analytics Engineer
-   BI / DWH Engineer
-   SQL / ETL roles presented as an AI transition

The existing data-engineering background is supporting evidence, not the
target identity.

## 2. Why this role

All three AI proposals converge on the same broad centre: applied AI
systems built with Python, LLMs, RAG/retrieval, agents/tool use,
evaluation, reliability and deployment. The main disagreement is how
much classical ML should be included and how quickly job applications
should start.

The selected target preserves the user's requirement for a future-facing
AI/ML/agentic role while using existing strengths instead of resetting
the career around traditional model-training work.

Current UK vacancies reviewed on 08/10/2026 reinforce this choice: roles
labelled Senior AI Engineer, Agentic AI Engineer, AI/ML Engineer ---
Agentic AI & LLMs, and Applied AI Engineer repeatedly ask for production
Python, RAG, tool calling/orchestration, evaluation, guardrails,
observability, security and cloud deployment.

## 3. Compare / contrast of the three teachers

### ChatGPT

**Direction:** Senior AI / Agentic Systems Engineer with strong
engineering foundations.\
**Strength:** production hardening --- RAG, agents, auth/OIDC, tenancy,
observability, evaluation, Azure, CI/CD, no-AI defence.\
**Risk:** can over-structure the curriculum and prematurely make the
three teachers converge.

### Claude

**Direction:** AI Engineer (LLM and agentic systems); Applied ML
Engineer second.\
**Strength:** strong insistence that the learner types/understands code
and can work independently.\
**Risk:** its initial attempt also tried to synchronise all three
teachers into one syllabus; that is explicitly rejected.

### Perplexity

**Direction:** applied AI / AI systems / agentic application
engineering, with ML foundations; ML engineering secondary pending a
readiness check.\
**Strength:** 130-hour dated preparation, evidence states, measured
evaluation, and an explicit classical-ML block rather than pretending
LLM application work equals ML engineering.\
**Risk:** four weekday hours may be heavier than necessary, and its role
recommendation was still provisional before the three-way comparison.

### Decision from comparison

Do **not** merge the teaching methods. Each AI remains an independent
teacher/mentor. They may choose different sequences, exercises,
explanations and even challenge the role decision.

The shared constraint is the employment destination and evidence
standard, not a shared syllabus.

## 4. Learning operating model

The learner is allowed to be a pure student in each teaching stream.

Each teacher may: - choose what to teach; - choose the order; - choose
exercises; - test differently; - revise its own plan; - disagree with
another teacher.

The workbench records what happened. It must not force artificial
consensus.

Periodically, the learner asks for cross-review. At that point
disagreements are compared against evidence, tests, market requirements
and actual learner performance.

## 6. Normal Git-turn protocol

Every AI session that changes the workbench follows the same visible
protocol:

1.  **START** --- timestamp, branch, `git status`, current objective.
2.  **PLAN** --- scoped work for this turn; assumptions; files expected
    to change.
3.  **WORK** --- learning/build/review performed.
4.  **VERIFY** --- commands/tests/evaluations and actual outputs.
5.  **DECISIONS** --- accepted/rejected/deferred decisions with reasons.
6.  **FILES** --- exact files changed/created.
7.  **COMMIT** --- scoped commit with meaningful message.
8.  **END** --- final `git status`, unresolved failures/blockers, next
    action.
9.  **SESSION LOG / HANDOVER** --- enough context for another AI to
    continue without relying on chat memory.

Never hide failures. Never mark proposed work as tested. Never mark
tested work as production. Never rely on an AI's confidence as evidence.

## 7. Workbench as future product seed

The workbench should initially solve the user's own context problem
rather than become a separate product-development distraction.

Evolution path:

**V0 Git truth** → session logs + decisions + evidence + artifacts\
**V1 Search** → metadata/full-text retrieval\
**V2 RAG** → semantic retrieval with citations/provenance\
**V3 Context graph** → link sessions, decisions, commits, claims, tests
and artifacts\
**V4 Tool/API layer** → allow AI clients/CLI/desktop/agents to retrieve
approved context\
**V5 Dashboard** → visualise learning, AI sessions, decisions, evidence,
disagreements and progress\
**V6 Model experiments** → evaluate whether SLM/PLM/fine-tuning adds
measurable value

Do not choose RAG vs SLM vs fine-tuning by fashion. Add each only when
an evaluation shows what problem it solves.

## 8. November gate

By 15 November, decide from evidence whether the learner is ready to
apply for the primary target. The decision is not based on finishing a
course. It is based on being able to build, debug, explain and defend
the core responsibilities without silently outsourcing understanding to
an AI.

The three AI teachers remain independent throughout.
