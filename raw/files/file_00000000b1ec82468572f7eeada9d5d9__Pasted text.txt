## Product risks to manage

Do not store raw recordings/transcripts indefinitely by default. Make retention, redaction, consent, access, and deletion tenant-configurable and contractually explicit.
Do not let AI scoring become an automatic disciplinary system for workers. Preserve human review, appeal, calibration, explanation, and policy/version provenance.
Do not place client API keys in prompts or application logs. Keep them in a tenant-scoped encrypted secret vault and invoke services through constrained tool endpoints.
Do not let an LLM directly access a broad CRM/database. Expose a small set of authenticated, auditable, purpose-specific actions such as find_customer, check_booking, create_callback, or handoff_case.
Do not couple TalkProof’s entire product to a single voice vendor. Use a provider-adapter boundary so Retell can be your first high-quality voice integration, not an irreplaceable architectural dependency.

I disagree with Perplexity's main recommendation. It describes a much bigger product, not a stronger position. I'd keep TalkProof as the proof layer and take only three ideas from it.

Where Perplexity goes wrong

It drops TalkProof's one real advantage. It never mentions the three-model consensus, the tamper-proof certificate, the blockchain anchor or the court-ready PDF. Your own 97-vendor analysis found that combination is what no competitor has, and it's already built and working in your code.
It describes features TalkProof doesn't have. It says TalkProof already includes QA scorecards, sampling, calibration, coaching, disputes, a case timeline and shift management. I searched the backend code, and none of those exist. Building them puts TalkProof up against the 19 contact-centre and QA vendors in your sheet (NICE, Calabrio, Playvox, Scorebuddy), which have had years of head start.
The roadmap doesn't fit where you are. Four phases toward a "BPO operating system" is years of work. TalkProof has no customers yet, and your own view is that distribution, not building, is the constraint. A wider product is harder to explain and harder to list in a marketplace.

What's worth keeping from it

Make Retell a swappable voice adapter. This matches my earlier "certify AI calls" point. It also fits your existing carrier pattern (S43), where Exotel and Twilio sit behind one interface. Treat Retell as one adapter, never a hard dependency.
Sell the AI ledger. TalkProof already records every AI call, by tenant, model, cost and outcome (S23), and neither Retell nor Delight.ai offers that to clients. It belongs in the sales story.
Don't let AI scores become discipline for workers. WET's workforce is women agents, so AI verdicts about an agent should always go through human review, with a right to appeal. Your existing reviewer gate already supports this; it just needs to be stated as policy.

My position: keep "TalkProof certifies what was said, by a human or an AI." Then take the three ideas above, but not the BPO operating system, the QA suite or the four-phase roadmap.
