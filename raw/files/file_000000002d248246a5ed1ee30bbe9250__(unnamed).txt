### The critical journey

The desired progression is:

**Pain → quantified cost → credible workflow → low-risk proof → measurable result → expansion → operational dependency**

That differs materially from selling “AI.”

### Where loyalty really comes from

[HYPOTHESIS] Loyalty won't come primarily from customer service. It should come from **workflow embedding**.

After 6–12 months, ORVIA ideally understands the customer's systems, workflow rules, exceptions, integrations, verification requirements and historical operational performance. That raises switching friction and can create accumulating value.

But **[UNKNOWN] whether ORVIA can create this effect strongly enough to produce retention.**

## Decision — ORVIA's first workflow

**TEST: Missed-Enquiry → Booked-Job Flow for UK field-service SMEs.**

[HYPOTHESIS] I would start with **property maintenance / multi-trade service businesses with roughly 5–30 employees**. This is narrower and more testable than “service SMEs.”

### The problem we sell

Customer calls, emails or messages while the team is busy.

**Today:**

**Call / WhatsApp / Email → human reads/listens → extracts details → decides priority → enters system/spreadsheet → replies → schedules → follows up**

ORVIA:

**Enquiry → capture → structure → qualify → route → draft/respond → book → track → human verification when needed**

The proposition is **not “AI automation.”**

> **Stop good enquiries disappearing into calls, messages and inboxes. ORVIA turns every enquiry into a tracked workflow from first contact to booked job.**

## Example economics

These are deliberately **assumptions**, not evidence.

Assume:

- 250 enquiries/month
- 8% poorly handled/missed = **20**
- 30% would have become jobs = **6 jobs**
- £300 average job = **£1,800 potential monthly revenue leakage**

If ORVIA recovered only **3 additional jobs**, that's **£900/month incremental revenue**.

That creates room to test perhaps **£199–£399/month** pricing—but **[UNKNOWN] willingness-to-pay has not been demonstrated**, and gross revenue recovered is not the same as customer profit.

### Why I chose this over generic workflow automation

It has a much cleaner chain:

**visible problem → measurable loss → automated intervention → measurable commercial outcome.**

It also creates an expansion path:

**Enquiry → Booking → Quote → Job → Invoice → Payment → Review/Repeat**

That could eventually make ORVIA the **workflow layer around the customer's existing systems**, rather than another standalone application.

### Biggest risk

[HYPOTHESIS] Existing field-service/job-management platforms may already solve enough of this problem that customers don't need ORVIA. **That competitive substitution question must be tested before building heavily.**
