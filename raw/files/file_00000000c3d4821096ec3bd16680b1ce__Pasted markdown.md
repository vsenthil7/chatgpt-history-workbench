### The videos are a business model, not a report

The "$62,000/month with Claude Code" genre follows one template: a creator builds something impressive on camera in 8 minutes, then shows a revenue number. The number is usually real. What it's revenue *from* is the part that gets skipped.

### "How do I find the customer" — the desk-research version

Here's the part that answers what you actually asked. Customer discovery without talking to anyone is not only possible on marketplaces, it's *better* than conversations, because the customers already wrote everything down.

**Competitor reviews are the interview transcripts.** Shopify's store alone holds 12.4M reviews. The 2- and 3-star reviews on the three apps closest to yours are a free, timestamped, unsolicited list of exactly what people need and aren't getting — in their own words, with their store type and use case attached. That is what 20 conversations would have produced, except it's 2,000 of them, already sorted, and nobody expects you to reply.

**Review counts are the demand meter.** You can't see installs, but review volume is a usable proxy. An app with 4,000 reviews in a category tells you the category has money in it. An app with 11 reviews after three years tells you it doesn't. You can rank your twelve products by category demand in an afternoon, without asking a single person.

**Marketplace search autocomplete is the keyword demand signal.** What merchants type is visible. That tells you both what to build toward and what to put in your listing title and subtitle — which, per the ranking research, is the *only* ranking factor you control directly. Everything else (install velocity, reviews, retention) only starts once you're discoverable.

### The math that argues for one, not twelve

Shopify's funnel runs roughly: 19% of listing visits install, and about **2.1% of visits become paying**. So \~100 paying customers needs \~4,700 listing visits. You get those visits by ranking. You rank on install velocity, review count, and retention — all of which **compound per app, and all of which are zero for an unlisted app.**

Twelve unlisted apps produce twelve zeros. One listed app starts a flywheel that feeds itself. Notably, \~80% of developers in that store have exactly one app. That's not a lack of ambition; it's what the ranking mechanics reward.
